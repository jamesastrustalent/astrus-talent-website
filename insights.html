Capex Watch

Microsoft Commits More Than $10 Billion to Gulf Cloud and AI Infrastructure Through 2030 Across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait, With an Additional $400 Million Allocated to New Subsea and Terrestrial Connectivity

Microsoft announced plans to invest more than $10 billion across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait through 2030, primarily in cloud and AI infrastructure, as Reuters reported and Data Center Dynamics covered on 24 September 2026. The commitment includes more than $400 million for new subsea and terrestrial connectivity across the Middle East, and was made in the context of the ongoing regional conflict, with Microsoft president Brad Smith stating the company is sustaining and adding to its planned Gulf investments.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  24 September 2026
 Market Intelligence

Iran Warns That Data Centers and AI Infrastructure Across the Gulf Could Be Targeted if War With the United States Resumes, After Earlier Strikes Damaged AWS Sites in the UAE and Bahrain

Iran's Supreme National Security Council warned that data centers and AI facilities across the Gulf region could face attack if hostilities with the United States resumed, Al Jazeera reported on 13 October 2026. The warning follows drone and missile strikes earlier in the year that damaged Amazon Web Services data centers in the UAE and Bahrain, and has prompted operators and investors to reassess resilience planning across the region as construction of major AI campuses continues at pace.

Source: Al Jazeera  ·  13 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Microsoft Exits Its Agreement With UK Startup Nscale at the Planned Loughton, Essex AI Data Center After London Grid Delays Mean Power May Not Be Available at the Site Until the Early 2030s

Microsoft confirmed it had withdrawn from an agreement to lease computing capacity at Nscale's planned AI data center in Loughton, Essex, after the London electricity grid was found unlikely to supply the site until the early to mid 2030s, as The Register reported on 19 October 2026. Nscale, which pitched the facility as the UK's largest sovereign AI data center, said the project is continuing as it explores alternative power sources, while its wider partnership with Microsoft across Norway, Portugal and the United States remains intact.

Source: The Register  ·  19 October 2026
 Capex Watch

Amazon Pledges More Than €16 Billion in Germany in 2027, With €4.8 Billion Earmarked for Expanding the AWS Europe Frankfurt Region, Announced at the Federal Government's Invest in Germany Summit

Amazon announced at the German federal government's Invest in Germany Summit in Berlin on 20 October 2026 that it plans to invest more than €16 billion in the country in 2027, with €4.8 billion allocated to expanding the AWS Europe (Frankfurt) Region, as Data Center Dynamics reported. The broader package also covers satellite internet, logistics, robotics and film production, and builds on what the company says is more than €100 billion of cumulative investment in Germany since 2010.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  20 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

NEXTDC Cuts FY27 Revenue Guidance by Around AU$140 Million and Shares Fall More Than 40 Per Cent After Utility Jemena Suspends Grid Works at Its Melbourne M3 Campus Due to Community Opposition

NEXTDC disclosed on 21 October 2026 that its utility Jemena Electricity Networks had suspended the 66kV grid augmentation works required to power its M3 campus in Melbourne's inner west, citing safety concerns and an absence of local council consent arising from community opposition, as RTT News reported. The resulting guidance revision reduced FY27 net revenue to AU$475 million to AU$500 million from AU$615 million to AU$640 million, shares fell to an eight year low, and the company paused all development projects not backed by signed customer contracts while expecting the next grid supply increase no earlier than FY2028.

Source: RTT News  ·  21 October 2026
 New Builds

NTT Data Is in Final Talks to Build Anthropic's First Dedicated Data Center in Japan, a Facility of Up to 500 MW in Osaka Estimated to Cost as Much as One Trillion Yen With Partial Operations Possible From 2028

NTT Data is in the final stages of talks with US AI company Anthropic to develop a data center of up to 500 MW in the bayside area of southern Osaka Prefecture at an estimated construction cost of up to one trillion yen ($6.3 billion), Nikkei Asia reported and Data Center Dynamics covered on 13 October 2026. The facility would be Anthropic's first dedicated data center in Japan, with both parties reportedly aiming to sign an agreement by March 2027, and the site is expected to generate its own power initially rather than relying on the grid given the scale of its electricity requirements.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  13 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Verizon Signs a Commercial Framework Agreement Valued at More Than $2 Billion With AWS to Supply Fibre Connectivity Between AI Data Centers, Following a Separate $1 Billion Deal With Google in July 2026

Verizon announced alongside its third quarter 2026 results on 26 October that it had signed a commercial framework agreement with Amazon Web Services valued at more than $2 billion, under which it will provide fibre connectivity between AWS data centers to support generative and agentic AI workloads, CNBC reported. The deal follows an earlier fibre agreement with Google worth more than $1 billion announced in July 2026, and signals a broader shift towards dedicated high capacity network infrastructure as hyperscalers look to reduce latency across large AI compute clusters.

Source: CNBC  ·  26 October 2026
 Capex Watch

Bank of America Global Research Forecasts That Global Data Center Spending Will Reach $1 Trillion in 2026 and More Than Double to $2.1 Trillion by 2028, With Servers the Single Largest Cost at Roughly $31 Million Per Megawatt

Bank of America Global Research published a forecast on 23 October 2026 projecting that global spending on data centers will reach approximately $1 trillion in 2026 before more than doubling to $2.1 trillion by 2028, with IT related items comprising roughly 78 per cent of total expenditure, as Reuters reported. The research identified electricity availability, equipment supply constraints, construction bottlenecks and community opposition as the principal risks to the build out, and placed the cost of servers at around $31.4 million per megawatt of data center capacity.

Source: Reuters  ·  23 October 2026

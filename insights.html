New Builds

G42 Leads Construction of 5GW AI Data Center Campus in Abu Dhabi for US Hyperscalers

Abu Dhabi based G42 is leading a consortium that includes OpenAI, Oracle and Nvidia to build what will be the largest AI data center campus outside the United States, covering 10 square miles and scaling to 5GW of capacity. The 200MW first phase of the 1GW initial cluster is targeting delivery by late 2026, with all long lead equipment procured and primary mechanical systems arriving on site.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  2026
 Capex Watch

Asprofin Bank Proposes Up to $12 Billion Sovereign Data Center Programme Across Three UAE Sites

Asprofin Bank Corporation has outlined a sovereign data center programme across three sites at Hatta, Jebel Ali and the Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Park, targeting between 750MW and 1,280MW of AI computing capacity across a 10 to 15 year phased timeline. The estimated investment of between $5 billion and $12 billion applies a modular architecture that Asprofin claims could deliver capacity at a 30 to 65 per cent cost advantage over conventional industry benchmarks.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  May 2026
 New Builds

Pure DC Launches €7.5 Billion Finland AI Campus with 110MW First Phase Fully Leased Before Ground Is Broken

Pure Data Centres Group has launched a 370 acre AI campus in Seinäjoki, Finland, committing over €1.5 billion to Phase 1 which delivers 110MW of AI ready capacity already fully leased before any construction begins. Microsoft is confirmed among the customers signed up, and the full programme is designed to scale to 550MW drawing on 700MVA of renewable power from the Finnish grid across a €7.5 billion total investment.

Source: Computer Weekly  ·  July 2026
 New Builds

EdgeMode and BlackBerry AIF Plan 300MW AI Campus in Toledo Running on Own Power as Spain Bids for Priority Status

EdgeMode and BlackBerry AIF have presented the DC Malpica AI project, a €3 billion, 300MW campus in Mora, Toledo, to Castilla-La Mancha officials as they seek Priority Project designation to accelerate planning approvals. The campus will use Bloom Energy fuel cells installed on site to generate its own electricity, removing dependence on the conventional grid, and is projected to create close to 5,000 jobs at peak construction.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  July 2026
 Market Intelligence

Japan Overtakes India as Asia Pacific Second Largest Data Center Market After Adding 293MW in H1 2026

Japan added 293MW of operational data center capacity in the first half of 2026, lifting its national total to 1.8GW and moving ahead of India as Asia Pacific's second largest operational market after mainland China, according to Cushman and Wakefield. Sustained AI and cloud investment continues to underpin demand, though power availability and land scarcity are increasingly determining where new capacity can be brought forward.

Source: Data Center Knowledge  ·  August 2026
 Market Intelligence

Malaysia Tops Southeast Asia with Over 1GW Under Construction as Johor Cements Hyperscale Dominance

Malaysia led Southeast Asia with 1,039MW under active construction as of mid-2026, cementing Johor's status as the subregion's dominant hyperscale corridor and challenging Singapore's long-standing position as the preferred destination for multinational operators. New supply across the subregion is being absorbed at pace, with a growing proportion of capacity committed to tenants before facilities reach completion.

Source: Data Center Knowledge  ·  August 2026
 New Builds

AWS Files $1.2 Billion Permit for Four Building Data Center Campus Southwest of Houston

Amazon Web Services has filed for Project Eagle, a $1.2 billion campus of four buildings in Boling, Wharton County, southwest of Houston, with construction commencing in August 2026 and the first three buildings targeted for completion by early 2027. The nearly 2,700 acre site is expected to become the largest data center facility in Texas when operational, with a peak power demand of approximately 75MW.

Source: Houston Chronicle  ·  August 2026
 Market Intelligence

Data Center Backlash Reaches the Ballot Box as 18 Officials Lose Seats Over Industry Votes

A Data Center Knowledge review of election results from August 2025 to August 2026 found that at least 18 incumbent officeholders lost their seats after taking documented action on a data center project, with challengers making electricity costs, land use and local control central to their campaigns. Governors in Texas, Pennsylvania and Ohio have responded by tightening approvals, reviewing tax incentives and in some cases pausing new projects entirely.

Source: Data Center Knowledge  ·  27 August 2026

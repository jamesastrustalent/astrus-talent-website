Market Intelligence

Siemens Energy CEO Says Saudi Arabia Can Power AI Data Centers Faster Than the United States as the Company Commits $150 Million to Expand Its Dammam Site and Add a New Power Transformer Factory for the Kingdom's 380 kV Grid

Siemens Energy CEO Christian Bruch told Asharq Bloomberg on 12 October 2026 that Saudi Arabia's upgraded transmission grid and coordinated capacity additions give it a timing advantage over the US for bringing AI data center capacity online, adding that moving a site's start date six months earlier or later could prove decisive in the global AI race. Siemens Energy is simultaneously expanding its Dammam site to approximately 75,000 square metres with a new large power transformer production line intended to supply equipment for Saudi Arabia's high voltage grid and export markets.

Source: Arab News  ·  12 October 2026
 Capex Watch

Microsoft Commits More Than $10 Billion to Data Centers, Cloud and AI Infrastructure Across Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE Through 2030, With Saudi Arabia's First Azure Region Due to Open in November

Announced at the UN General Assembly on 23 September 2026, the Gulf framework covers capital and operating expenditure across four markets, with a three availability zone Saudi Azure region scheduled to open in November 2026 and a G42 partnership in the UAE set to deliver an additional 200 MW of capacity before year end. Microsoft said more than $400 million of the investment is allocated to subsea and terrestrial connectivity, framing the commitment around digital resilience and data protection programmes following the regional conflict earlier in 2026.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  24 September 2026
 Regulation

Finland Orders a Halt to Construction Work at Two Google Data Center Sites After Around 530 Hectares of Forest Were Cleared Without Mandatory Environmental Impact Assessments in Muhos and Kajaani

Finland's environmental regulator LVV issued the halt order on 7 October 2026, directing Google's Finnish subsidiary Tuike Finland to stop tree removal, excavation, blasting and road construction at both sites by 23 October and to complete environmental impact assessment reports before work can resume. Google acknowledged it fell short of its own standards and said it had acted in good faith under the Forestry Act, while the regulator warned that enforcement proceedings would follow if Tuike Finland failed to comply with the deadline.

Source: Al Jazeera  ·  7 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Nscale Secures $3.36 Billion in Pre IPO Convertible Financing Led by Third Point With Nvidia, Apollo and Abu Dhabi Investment Council Among Investors, but Flagship Data Center Delays Raise Questions About Neocloud Execution Risk

The London based AI cloud and data center company closed its convertible loan note offering on 25 September 2026 and is targeting a US IPO that may raise up to $3 billion, with contracted revenue across the portfolio reported at roughly $51 billion and active and contracted power standing at approximately 831 MW. Bloomberg reported on 16 October that the company's flagship data center has hit construction delays ahead of listing, pointing to broader risks facing neocloud operators whose business models depend on delivering capacity at the scale and pace their customer agreements require.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  16 October 2026
 Capex Watch

Blackstone Backed AirTrunk Commits an Additional $1 Billion to Its Inzai Campus Near Tokyo to Retrofit Liquid Cooling for AI Workloads, Backed by a $1 Billion Green Loan From SMBC, MUFG and United Overseas Bank

AirTrunk announced on 7 October 2026 that the additional capital will enable its flagship Japanese campus, which exceeds 300 MW, to host large scale AI operations and become the first facility in Japan capable of supporting that class of workload. CEO Robin Khuda cited growing customer demand for AI capable capacity in Asia driven partly by geopolitical constraints on US and European buildouts, with APAC increasingly seen as a beneficiary of shifting AI infrastructure investment.

Source: Reuters  ·  7 October 2026
 Regulation

Thailand Targets Mid October to Finalise New Data Center Regulations Classifying Facilities Above 100 MW as Hyperscale and Requiring Industrial Estate Locations, With 49 Active Projects Suspended and 117 Planned Facilities Frozen

Thailand's National Data Center Business Policy Committee is finalising a framework that categorises data centers into size bands based on electricity consumption, with the 100 MW threshold triggering a hyperscale designation that restricts permissible locations and triggers a dedicated electricity tariff. Construction work at all active sites was suspended and approvals for 117 planned facilities were frozen while the framework was drafted, putting approximately 166 projects on hold pending the new rules.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  October 2026
 New Builds

Digital Realty Plans Its First Indiana Campus Near Kokomo With Three Buildings Spanning 476 Acres and Around 1 GW of IT Capacity, Committing to Fund All Grid Upgrades Without Seeking Local Tax Abatements

Digital Realty confirmed in mid October 2026 that the campus, roughly 50 miles north of Indianapolis in Howard County, would deliver approximately 460 MW per building across three phases with full buildout targeted by 2033, requiring around 1.5 GW of total power draw. The developer is not requesting tax abatements and has proposed closed loop cooling to reduce water consumption, with an economic development agreement with Kokomo Common Council expected in the coming weeks.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  16 October 2026
 Policy

Virginia Senator Proposes a Three Year Statewide Data Center Moratorium Starting July 2027 as Loudoun County Enacts Its Own 12 Month Pause and Industry Backlash Shapes the 2026 Midterm Elections

Democratic senator Russet Perry introduced Senate Bill 843 on 15 October 2026, which would halt state agency approvals of data center planning applications for three years from July 2027, giving lawmakers time to draft permanent rules; separately, Loudoun County's board of supervisors voted 7 to 1 in September to pause all new data center applications for 12 months. Nationally, moratorium proposals have been introduced in 11 states in 2026 and the issue has become a significant theme in the midterm election cycle, with campaigns spending heavily on data center related messaging.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  15 October 2026

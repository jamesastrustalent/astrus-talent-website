New Builds

Khazaen Data Center Unveils Plans for a 45 Megawatt AI Campus in Oman's Khazaen Economic City, With Room to Expand to 100 Megawatts and a Target of Q2 2028 for the First Operational Phase

Khazaen Data Center announced a carrier neutral AI campus in Barka, northern Oman, occupying a 55,000 square metre site within the Khazaen Economic City and designed with an initial 45 megawatts of IT capacity and room to expand to 100 megawatts in later phases. Backed by the Al Adrak Group, the project targets a first operational phase in Q2 2028 and is seeking international hyperscalers, cloud operators and AI developers from Europe, North America and India.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  2 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Microsoft Narrows Its Saudi Arabia East Azure Region Launch to November 2026, Bringing Three Availability Zones to the Eastern Province as AWS Prepares a Separate Saudi Region for December

Microsoft confirmed a November 2026 go live for its Saudi Arabia East Azure region in the Eastern Province, designed around three availability zones with independent power, cooling and networking infrastructure and pitched primarily for data residency and government workloads. Google and Oracle already operate national cloud regions in Saudi Arabia, and AWS has separately committed to opening its first Saudi cloud region in December, completing the main hyperscaler presence in the Kingdom.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Up to 200 People Detained After Protesters Break Through the Fence of Microsoft's Amsterdam Data Center Construction Site and Climb a Crane in the Opening Action of a Two Day European Anti Data Center Campaign

Activists from the Dutch group Geef Tegengas broke through a security fence at Microsoft's disputed Amsterdam data center site on 6 November 2026, with some climbing a construction crane and setting off flares before Dutch police detained between 100 and 200 people for trespassing. The site occupies a legal grey area because its planning application predates Amsterdam's ban on new data centers in 2016, and provincial authorities subsequently allowed the project to proceed; it has now been targeted in three separate protest actions since construction began.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  6 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Norway's Grid Operator Statnett Pauses New Data Center Grid Connections Until August 2027 as the Government Introduces a Mandatory Licensing Regime to Prioritise Critical Infrastructure Over AI Facilities

Statnett suspended new grid capacity reservations for data centers on 9 November 2026, citing the need to finalise technical standards governing how facilities behave during faults and manage load shifts, while the Norwegian government simultaneously announced that any operator wishing to build a new data center in the country will require an authorisation licence with conditions attached. The pipeline of pending connection requests is estimated at several gigawatts against approximately 800 megawatts currently connected, and defence, emergency services and healthcare facilities are expected to receive priority in future approvals.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Malaysia Mandates a 30 Per Cent Minimum Renewable Energy Requirement for All Data Centers as the Sector Approaches 10 Per Cent of National Electricity Demand With Projections Reaching 31 Per Cent by 2035

Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof announced on 28 October 2026 that data centers in Malaysia must source at least 30 per cent of their electricity from renewables, to be satisfied through the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme or solar self consumption programmes, with the Energy Commission still finalising detailed guidelines. The rule reflects government concern that the sector already accounts for close to 10 per cent of Malaysia's total electricity consumption, a share modelled to reach 31 per cent by 2035 if growth continues at its current pace.

Source: The Edge Malaysia  ·  28 October 2026
 New Builds

Zhi Sheng Group's Thai AI Data Center Secures a $590 Million Seven Year Bank Loan Backed by a $1.6 Billion Colocation Agreement as the Eastern Economic Corridor Campus Targets a First Phase in 2027

Haoyang Data Centre 1, a hyperscale AI campus under development in Thailand's Eastern Economic Corridor, secured a seven year loan of approximately 590 million US dollars from a Thai bank in November 2026 to fund construction, underpinned by a ten year colocation agreement signed in July with a leading internet company and valued at about 1.6 billion US dollars excluding electricity. Developer Zhi Sheng Group, which agreed to acquire the project in September, is targeting the first phase of the facility for delivery within six months.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  5 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Voters Back Data Center Restrictions in 40 of 41 Local Ballot Measures on 3 November as Five Outright Bans Pass Across California, Florida and Ohio and the Senate Advances Bipartisan Permitting Reform

Ballotpedia counted 41 local ballot measures on data center development across ten US states on 3 November 2026 and found voters supported restrictions in 40 of them, covering five outright bans in California, Florida and Ohio, three six month moratoriums in Maine and 32 other limiting measures. The results came in the same week the Senate advanced a bipartisan permitting reform bill 73 to 25, with provisions on electricity costs for large data centers, as analysts warned that more than half of US hyperscale projects now face significant regulatory friction.

Source: Ballotpedia  ·  6 November 2026
 New Builds

Google Commits $17 Billion to Its First Wyoming Data Center Complex Near Cheyenne, With Air Cooling, 702 Megawatts of Bloom Energy Fuel Cells and a Pledge to Cover 100 Per Cent of Its Power Use

Google announced on 5 November 2026 that it will invest 17 billion US dollars in a data center complex in Laramie County, Wyoming, developed with Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power and Related Digital, with a 702 megawatt Bloom Energy fuel cell plant providing primary power under a 20 year take or pay contract and air cooling technology protecting local water supplies. Black Hills is expected to begin service in late 2027 with ramp to full demand by 2030, as the project arrived in the same week that US voters backed data center restrictions in 40 of 41 counted local ballot measures.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  5 November 2026

Market Intelligence

US Threat to Tax Overseas AI Data Center Builds Could Push Gulf States Toward Home Grown Capability, as HUMAIN Expands Its Nvidia Partnership and Analysts Argue the Measure May Deepen Rather Than Disrupt American Technology Relationships in the Region

Reporting published today in The National argues that President Trump's threatened tax on US companies building data centers outside the United States may push Saudi Arabia and the UAE to accelerate their own AI capacity rather than wait on foreign funded builds. HUMAIN, the Public Investment Fund backed AI company, recently expanded its Nvidia partnership to cover an 84MW AI computing project, and analysts told the paper that the administration's measure could ultimately encourage Gulf states to build sovereign computing capability as a hedge against US regulatory uncertainty.

Source: The National  ·  26 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund Signs Two Financing Agreements With HUMAIN Worth Up to SAR 2.5 Billion, Covering GPU Procurement for a 25MW Initial Deployment and Transmission Infrastructure for the Country's Wider AI Data Center Expansion

Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund (Infra) and HUMAIN signed two agreements in Riyadh on 8 November marking the first transactions under Infra's AI Data Center Financing Programme. A GPU facility of up to SAR 1.1 billion funds chip procurement and deployment for approximately 25MW of IT load, while a second facility of up to SAR 1.4 billion covers substations and grid connections for HUMAIN's planned campuses. The deals follow Aramco completing its acquisition of a 49 per cent stake in HUMAIN on 1 November, with the Public Investment Fund retaining 51 per cent.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Norway's Transmission System Operator Statnett Halts All New Data Center Grid Connection Applications Until at Least August 2027, as Queued Demand of More Than 9,000MW Already Exceeds the Country's National Peak Electricity Load

Statnett paused all new grid capacity reservations for data centers on 9 November while the government designs a licensing scheme to prioritise defence, health, telecoms and financial services over cryptocurrency mining and speculative AI capacity. The company reports approximately 9,100MW of data center requests in the pipeline against only 800MW currently connected to the Norwegian grid, while the country's national peak demand stands at around 25,000MW. Projects already holding grid approval are unaffected by the pause.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Quantitative Trading Firm XTX Markets Opens Its First Owned Data Center in Kajaani, Finland, With 22.5MW of Initial Capacity on a 193 Hectare Campus Earmarked for More Than €1 Billion in Total Investment and a Second Building Due in 2027

XTX Markets launched its inaugural data center in Kajaani in late November, taking an unusual step for a financial services company by owning and operating its own large scale compute campus rather than leasing space from a commercial provider. The site, built by construction firm YIT on a 193 hectare plot in northern Finland, adds to a growing cluster of hyperscale and specialist facilities in the region drawn by low temperatures, low electricity prices and renewable power availability.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

S&P Global Ratings Warns That Asia Pacific Data Center Operators Face Elevated Credit Risks After New US Chip Export Controls Take Effect, With Operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia Now Required to Obtain Licences for Advanced Nvidia Hardware

S&P Global Ratings said on 25 November that Asia Pacific data center operators carry the greatest exposure to the interim US export control rule that became effective on 19 November, citing the region's high dependence on Chinese customers and the stricter licensing regime now covering four Southeast Asian markets. Cloud providers in the affected countries have a 90 day window to secure initial licences, and S&P noted that the uncertainty is already affecting project financing discussions across the region.

Source: TechNode Global  ·  25 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Alibaba Cloud Opens Its Sixth Data Center in Malaysia, the Third Facility in the Johor Cloud Region, Making Malaysia Its Largest Data Center Base in Southeast Asia Across a Global Footprint of 109 Availability Zones in 32 Regions

Alibaba Cloud launched its sixth Malaysian data center on 20 November at an event in Kuala Lumpur, adding capacity to its Johor cloud region for AI workloads and to meet Malaysian data residency requirements. Malaysia is now the company's largest data center base in Southeast Asia, and Alibaba said the new facility serves growing demand from banking, telecoms and digital payments operators that require local processing under Malaysian data governance rules.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

The US Army Invites Private Proposals to Develop AI Data Centers Across Nine Military Sites on the Condition That Every Bid Includes On Site Power Generation, Extending a Federal Push to Mobilise Private Capital Inside Secure Government Perimeters

The US Army released a request for proposals covering nine military sites where private companies may bid to develop and operate AI data centers, with on site power generation mandatory in all submissions and power only bids also accepted. The initiative follows a comparable offer by NASA at Ames Research Center and reflects a federal strategy to draw private AI investment onto existing government land where security perimeters and potential grid connections reduce development costs and timelines.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  25 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Core Scientific Files a USD 1.3 Billion Data Center Plan in Hunt County, Texas, With AMD as Anchor Tenant for 110MW Under a 15 Year Agreement, Adding to a Total of 377MW of AMD Contracted Capacity Across Three US States

Core Scientific registered plans with Texas regulators for a 285MW campus in Hunt County, northeast of Dallas, at an estimated cost of USD 1.3 billion with Gensler as the design firm and a construction window running from November 2026 to September 2028. AMD has signed a 15 year lease covering approximately 110MW at Hunt County, part of a broader 377MW commitment from AMD spanning facilities in Pecos, Texas; Muskogee, Oklahoma; and Hunt County, with first power delivery expected in the first half of 2028.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026

Market Intelligence

Analysts Ask Whether UAE Data Center Demand Can Keep Pace With a Pipeline of More Than Five Gigawatts of New Capacity as Microsoft Opens Its Saudi Arabia East Azure Region to Customers in November 2026

The National News reported on 20 November 2026 that economists and developers across the Gulf are questioning whether AI driven workloads can absorb the volume of new capacity being announced, following confirmation that Microsoft's Saudi Arabia East Azure region is now available to customers across the Kingdom's Eastern Province, with Analysys Mason forecasting accelerated Gulf data center investment of between five and seven billion dollars in 2026. Some analysts cautioned that announced capacity targets across Abu Dhabi, Saudi Arabia and beyond may outpace near term demand, as the volume of projects under development has grown faster than committed customer contracts.

Source: The National News  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Export Controls and Nvidia's Leadership Crisis Expose the Gulf's Dependence on American Technology Policy as Washington's AI Security Decisions Put Billions in Saudi and UAE Infrastructure Commitments at Risk

Foreign Policy reported on 19 November 2026 that the upheaval at Nvidia following Jensen Huang's resignation, combined with new US remote access licensing requirements, highlights how vulnerable Gulf AI programmes are to decisions made in Washington, with Humain contracted to deploy 27,000 GPUs in Saudi Arabia and Stargate UAE on course to build a one gigawatt AI cluster in Abu Dhabi. Analysts told Foreign Policy that the UAE's strategy of building deep partnerships with American technology companies offers some protection, while Saudi Arabia's focus on sovereign compute leaves it more exposed to any further US policy shifts.

Source: Foreign Policy  ·  19 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Norway's Grid Operator Statnett Halts All New Data Center Grid Reservations Until August 2027 as the Government Announces Plans for a Mandatory Licensing Regime to Control the Country's Surging Power Demand

Statnett announced on 9 November 2026 that it was pausing new grid capacity reservations for data centers, citing an active queue of 9,100 megawatts of reserved demand and a further 18,000 megawatts in outstanding enquiries, against only 800 megawatts of connected data center load currently operating in the country. The Norwegian government responded by announcing plans for a formal licensing regime for data center operators, with the pause expected to remain in force until at least 1 August 2027 and industry analysts tracking more than 74 active projects in Norway worth over US$39 billion in investment.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Spain's Government Seeks Fast Track Ministerial Approval for a Decree Requiring Data Centers to Source 80 Per Cent of Electricity From Renewables on an Hourly Basis Before the 29 November General Election

The proposed decree, which the government plans to pass without parliamentary approval, would require facilities to match 80 per cent of their hourly consumption with renewable energy, prompting industry body SpainDC to warn it would create the most restrictive data center regulatory framework in the European Union. CBRE estimates that only 4.5 of the 10.5 gigawatts of announced capacity across Spain and Portugal is realistically deliverable given existing grid and permitting constraints, while the Aragón regional government called the draft a de facto prohibition on new construction.

Source: CNBC  ·  17 November 2026
 New Builds

SoftBank, Grab and Petroleum Sarawak Sign Binding Joint Development Agreements Committing US$1.85 Billion in Seed Capital to Build AI Data Center Infrastructure Across Malaysia Targeting More Than Five Gigawatts of Capacity

The three companies announced on 9 November 2026 that they had signed binding joint development agreements for combined seed capital of US$1.85 billion, comprising US$1 billion from SoftBank Group, US$750 million from Grab and US$100 million from Petroleum Sarawak, with investment to be deployed in stages as development milestones and customer contracts are achieved across sites in Sarawak and Peninsular Malaysia. The partners plan to bring in additional investors and hyperscaler customers to support expansion towards the long term target of more than five gigawatts of AI computing infrastructure.

Source: The Asian Banker  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Southeast Asia's Data Center Boom Faces a Structural Threat as US Commerce Department Licensing Rules Require Operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia to Obtain Approval Before Purchasing Advanced AI Chips

CNBC reported on 18 November 2026 that the interim licensing rule released by the US Commerce Department closes a loophole that had made Southeast Asia a destination for Chinese technology companies seeking access to computing capacity above thresholds defined by earlier export controls, with analysts warning it could redirect investment flows across the region as developers reassess projects that relied in part on Chinese customers. Thailand's foreign minister said the country must now be more careful in attracting data center investment, while one Singapore based firm's 480 million dollar GPU financing package stalled directly as a result of the new requirement.

Source: CNBC  ·  18 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Loudoun County, Virginia Enacts a Twelve Month Pause on New Data Center Applications, Becoming the First Jurisdiction to Restrict Development in the World's Largest and Most Concentrated Data Center Market

The Loudoun County Board of Supervisors voted to enact a twelve month pause on legislative approvals for new data center and substation applications in November 2026, launching a formal review of the county's approach to development that is expected to conclude in summer 2027. Industry groups warned the pause would affect Loudoun's status as the world's most concentrated data center market and said the measure could face legal challenge over the county's authority to delay applications already in progress under Virginia law.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026

Core Scientific Files a US$1.3 Billion Development Plan for a 285 Megawatt Hunt County Campus in Texas With AMD Leasing 110 Megawatts Under a Fifteen Year Agreement as Part of a US$14 Billion Multi Campus Partnership

Core Scientific registered development plans in November 2026 for a multi building data center campus across 265 acres in Quinlan, Hunt County, northeast of Dallas, with AMD contracted to take 110 megawatts of leasable capacity under a fifteen year agreement and holding a reservation right for up to 1,925 megawatts of additional capacity across the developer's portfolio through December 2028. The Hunt County campus forms part of a broader US$14 billion partnership between Core Scientific and AMD signed in July 2026, covering 530 megawatts across five campuses with an expansion option of up to 2.5 gigawatts.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026

Market Intelligence

UAE Government Cybersecurity Chief Declares Data Centers Critical National Infrastructure at the Inaugural Data Center World Middle East Conference in Abu Dhabi and Calls for Sector Wide Protection From the Design Stage

Dr Mohamed Al Kuwaiti, the UAE government's head of cybersecurity, told the first Middle East edition of Data Center World in Abu Dhabi on 17 November that data centers must be treated as sovereign assets and secured from the design stage, with protection extending to substations, cable routes, subsea connections and any vendor with remote access. He said the rapid AI infrastructure expansion in the UAE requires a coordinated approach to power, security and architecture, drawing on the country's experience responding to attacks on critical facilities earlier in 2026.

Source: Gulf Business  ·  18 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

New Zealand Infrastructure Investor Morrison Opens an Abu Dhabi ADGM Office and Says It Is Actively Seeking UAE Partners to Jointly Invest in Renewable Energy and AI Data Center Projects Across Global Markets

Morrison, a New Zealand based infrastructure investment manager, opened an office in Abu Dhabi's ADGM financial centre on 19 November and said it aims to identify UAE sovereign and institutional investors willing to jointly develop renewable energy and AI data center projects globally. The move reflects growing interest from international capital in the Gulf as a joint investment partner for large scale AI infrastructure expansion, as Abu Dhabi consolidates its position as the largest data center market in the Middle East with 374 megawatts of operational IT capacity.

Source: The National  ·  19 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Norway's Grid Operator Statnett Suspends New Data Center Grid Connection Applications Until August 2027 as a Surge in Demand Threatens Power System Stability and the Government Prepares a Licensing Regime

Statnett, Norway's national grid operator, announced on 9 November that it would halt the processing of new grid capacity applications from data center developers, saying rapid growth in electricity demand from the sector risked destabilising the power system. The pause is due to remain in place until 1 August 2027, when the government expects to introduce a licensing scheme that would favour facilities serving critical national functions such as defence, health services and emergency response over general commercial and AI data center development.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

European Union Enlargement Commissioner Proposes to Build a Sovereign Data Center for Moldova to Protect Citizens' Personal Data From Cyberattacks and Reduce the Country's Dependence on Uncontrolled Infrastructure

The EU enlargement commissioner offered to build and operate a data center for Moldova during a visit to the country, proposing the facility as a means of keeping Moldovan citizens' personal data protected from cyberattacks and outside interference. The proposal forms part of broader EU efforts to extend trusted digital infrastructure to candidate countries, as Moldova seeks to anchor its critical systems to European sovereign technology frameworks.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Commerce Department Issues Interim Rule Requiring Licences for Advanced AI Chip Shipments to Data Centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia, Effective 19 November, to Close Remote Access Loopholes for Chinese Firms

The US Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on 16 November requiring data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia to obtain a government licence before receiving advanced AI chips from US manufacturers including Nvidia, with the rule taking effect on 19 November. The controls target a loophole allowing Chinese firms to access US chip compute by renting capacity at overseas facilities, with analysts expecting delays and higher costs for data center development across Southeast Asia rather than an end to the region's AI infrastructure expansion.

Source: CNBC  ·  18 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

At Least Two Nvidia Server Assemblers Suspend Southeast Asia Shipments After US Export Controls Take Effect, Sending Shares of Malaysian Data Center Operators Down Nearly 20 Per Cent

At least two of Nvidia's contracted server assemblers halted shipments to clients in Southeast Asia following the introduction of US chip export licensing requirements on 19 November, according to Nikkei Asia. Malaysian listed data center operators MN Holdings and Critical Holdings both fell nearly 20 per cent on the announcement, while Singapore moved to defend its status as a trusted technology hub and Malaysia's trade minister said companies applying to build data centers would need to disclose operators, sites and planned GPU imports.

Source: Nikkei Asia  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

President Trump Threatens a Tremendous Tax on US Companies That Build AI Data Centers Outside the United States as Alphabet and Amazon Shares Fall on Investor Concern Over the Policy Implications

President Donald Trump threatened on 20 November to impose what he called a tremendous tax on US companies including Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft that build AI data centers outside the United States, saying they could do so but would face the levy if they did. No rate, legal mechanism or executive order accompanied the statement as of 21 November, though Alphabet and Amazon shares each fell 3 to 4 per cent on the news, coming less than three weeks after voter concern over data center development contributed to Republican losses in the midterm elections.

Source: CNBC  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Turner & Townsend Finds US Data Center Construction Costs Rose 13 Per Cent in 2026, More Than Double the Global Rate and Triple the Decade Average, as Demand for AI Workloads Outpaces Powered Land and Skilled Labour

A Turner & Townsend report examining more than 500 projects across 62 global markets found that US data center construction costs increased 13 per cent year on year in 2026, more than twice the global rate and roughly triple the decade long average, driven by powered land shortages, competition for skilled labour and contractors pricing in greater risk on aggressive build schedules. The firm's North America lead said cost growth was expected to moderate to between 6 and 10 per cent in the coming year, while noting that AI specific facilities cost around 10 per cent more than traditional data centers in the US market.

Source: CNBC  ·  20 November 2026

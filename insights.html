Market Intelligence

AWS Confirms It Will Not Restore Access to Data Center Infrastructure in the UAE and Bahrain Struck by Iranian Drones, With the Bahrain Recovery Timeline Deferred to Early 2027

Amazon Web Services has confirmed it will not be restoring access to data center infrastructure at its facilities in the UAE and Bahrain damaged by Iranian strikes, with the company stating it will share further details about its Bahrain timeline in early 2027. The original March 2026 drone strikes took two of the three availability zones in the AWS Middle East Central 1 region offline, and subsequent attacks on the Bahrain site and a claimed IRGC cruise missile strike in July 2026 have left regional cloud customers managing extended outages across the Gulf.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Commerce Department Reclassifies the UAE Into Its Top Export Control Tier, Removing Individual Licence Requirements for Nvidia and AMD AI Chips and Benefiting G42, Core42 and American Hyperscalers Alike

The US Commerce Department has reclassified the UAE into Country Group A:5, eliminating individual export licence requirements for advanced AI processors from Nvidia and AMD and extending the same benefit to US hyperscalers running data centers in the country, including Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle. The reclassification was partly framed as recognition of the UAE's support for the United States during its ongoing conflict with Iran and strengthens the UAE's position as the Gulf's leading AI infrastructure hub.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Thousands March Against AI Data Centers in Dublin, Helsinki and Milan as a Coordinated European Campaign Involving Over 100 Groups Builds on the Amsterdam Site Invasion of 6 November

More than 100 campaign groups organised anti-data center marches across Europe on 6 and 7 November 2026, drawing approximately 2,000 demonstrators in Dublin and around 1,300 in Helsinki as part of the Stop Dirty Data Centres campaign, with thousands more taking to the streets in Milan on 10 November. The coordinated action followed the Amsterdam protest in which up to 200 people broke through the security fence of Microsoft's disputed construction site, and encompasses groups in the UK, Netherlands, Spain, Ireland, Poland, Croatia, Denmark, Finland and France targeting both specific projects and the national planning frameworks that have permitted rapid data center expansion.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  7 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

The European Central Bank Is Weighing a Targeted Review of Eurozone Bank Lending to Data Center Projects, Examining Cash Flow Modelling and Collateral Valuations as Supervisory Concern Grows

Senior European Central Bank officials have reportedly floated the idea internally and with supervised institutions of a focused review into data center lending, prompted by concern that rapid expansion in this area could leave banks exposed if AI valuations soften or community opposition to new builds intensifies. Any review is expected to be narrower in scope than the ECB's earlier leveraged finance exercise but would involve on site inspections examining how banks project future cash flows, value collateral and provision for defaults, and no final approach has yet been announced.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  6 November 2026
 New Builds

Equinix Opens the $290 Million SG6 Data Center in Singapore Four Months Ahead of Schedule, Bringing 8 Megawatts of Capacity for AI Workloads Under the Framework That Followed Singapore's Data Center Moratorium

Equinix has opened SG6 in Singapore ahead of its original Q1 2027 schedule, delivering 8 megawatts of IT capacity and approximately 1,550 cabinets on an initial investment of roughly 290 million US dollars, with the facility designed to reach 20 megawatts at full build-out. SG6 was developed under Singapore's Data Centre Call for Application framework and supports liquid cooling capability for power intensive GPU deployments, with a joint innovation facility with the National University of Singapore planned for launch in 2027.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  6 November 2026
 New Builds

SoftBank, Grab and PETROS Sign a Joint Development Agreement for AI Data Centers in Sarawak With $1 Billion in Initial Seed Capital and an Ambition to Develop More Than 5 Gigawatts of Compute Across Malaysia

SoftBank, Grab and PETROS signed a joint development agreement on 9 November 2026 to build AI data center infrastructure in Sarawak, Malaysia, with initial seed capital of approximately 1 billion US dollars and SoftBank contributing 800 million dollars alongside Grab and PETROS. The Sarawak commitment forms part of a broader ambition to eventually develop more than 5 gigawatts of AI compute capacity across Malaysia, as the country attracts significant hyperscale capital despite its total installed data center power capacity of only around 1.3 gigawatts as of mid-2026.

Source: The Edge Malaysia  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

BloombergNEF Finds That On Site Gas Plants Proposed for US Data Centers Would Emit Around 345 Million Metric Tonnes of CO2 Each Year, Adding Approximately 10 Per Cent to 2025 US Power Sector Emissions

A BloombergNEF report commissioned by Bloomberg Philanthropies and published on 11 November 2026 found that the 165 gigawatts of on site gas generation proposed alongside US data centers would emit approximately 345 million metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent annually, representing roughly 10 per cent of total 2025 US power sector emissions. Data center developers are turning to on site generation because grid connection queues cannot accommodate AI infrastructure deployment timelines, with more than 90 per cent of the 181 gigawatts of announced on site gas capacity globally concentrated in the United States.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  11 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Governors Elected on Promises to Pause Data Center Construction Now Oversee Roughly 40 Per Cent of the US Development Pipeline, With Bloomberg Intelligence Estimating a 5 to 7 Per Cent Cut to 2027 AI Infrastructure Spending

Analysis by Cleanview cited by Bloomberg estimated that newly elected governors in Texas, Ohio, Georgia and six other states who campaigned on pledges to stop or pause data center construction oversee roughly 41 per cent of the US planned capacity pipeline, with Texas alone accounting for approximately 88 gigawatts of that total. Bloomberg Intelligence analysts separately estimated the November 2026 election results could reduce planned 2027 AI infrastructure spending by 5 to 7 per cent and delay up to 10 gigawatts of capacity from coming online as scheduled, as nearly three in four Americans now say they oppose a data center in their community.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  6 November 2026

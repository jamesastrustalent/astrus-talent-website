Market Intelligence

Microsoft Opens Its Saudi Arabia East Data Center Region to Cloud Customers in November 2026, Giving Saudi Organisations Access to Azure and AI Services Hosted Entirely Within the Kingdom for the First Time Across Three Independent Availability Zones

Microsoft's Saudi Arabia East data center region became available to customers in November 2026, allowing Saudi government and private sector organisations to run eligible Azure cloud and AI workloads locally without data leaving the kingdom. The region is located in the Eastern Province and is built across three availability zones, each with its own independent power, cooling and networking infrastructure, and a companion Microsoft Innovation Hub opened in Saudi Arabia at the same time.

Source: Microsoft  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Analysts Question Whether Nvidia Leadership Transition Will Disrupt Gulf AI Data Center Ambitions as Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN and Abu Dhabi's G42 Hold Significant Commitments Tied Closely to Nvidia's Sovereign AI Partnerships

Fortune reported on 18 November that analysts are assessing the potential impact of an Nvidia leadership transition on the Gulf's AI data center expansion plans, noting that personal relationships between the former chief executive and Gulf leaders had underpinned agreements including HUMAIN's AI factory programme in Saudi Arabia and the G42 supercluster in Abu Dhabi. Analysts said existing contracts appeared secure but warned that replicating sovereign AI relationships at the same pace would be difficult, while US export controls remained a separate watchpoint for the region.

Source: Fortune  ·  18 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

More Than 100 Groups Across 15 Countries Stage Coordinated European Protests on 6 and 7 November Demanding a Moratorium on Large Scale Data Center Construction, With Thousands Marching in Dublin and Around 1,300 Rallying in Helsinki

The Stop Dirty Data Centres campaign held coordinated protests across Europe on 6 and 7 November, drawing thousands of demonstrators in Dublin, around 1,300 people in Helsinki and further actions in Ireland, Poland, Denmark, Finland, Spain, the Netherlands, France and the United Kingdom. Campaigners called for moratoriums at local, national and European levels, arguing that unchecked expansion was driving up energy and water demand without adequate public scrutiny, while some Members of the European Parliament said Europe needed more data center capacity to remain competitive in AI.

Source: The Register  ·  5 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

EU Cloud and AI Development Act Sets a Target to Triple European Data Center Capacity Within Five to Seven Years at a Cost of Around 200 Billion Euros, as Member States Face Competing Pressures From AI Ambitions and Environmental Opposition

Euronews Verify reported on 17 November that the EU's Cloud and AI Development Act, published in June 2026, commits the bloc to tripling its data center capacity within five to seven years to reduce reliance on US technology companies and support European AI development. The European Data Centre Association said the investment was essential for competitiveness, while environmental campaigners and water resource groups warned that expansion on this scale would worsen energy and water pressures already evident in Spain, Ireland and the Netherlands.

Source: Euronews  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Commerce Department Issues Interim Rule Requiring Licences for Advanced AI Chip Shipments to Data Centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia, Effective 19 November, to Close Remote Access Loopholes for Chinese Firms

The US Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on 16 November requiring data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia to obtain a government licence before receiving advanced AI chips from US manufacturers including Nvidia, with the rule taking effect on 19 November. The controls target a loophole allowing Chinese firms to access US chip compute by renting capacity at overseas facilities, with analysts expecting delays and higher costs for data center development across Southeast Asia rather than an end to the region's AI infrastructure expansion.

Source: CNBC  ·  18 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

At Least Two Nvidia Server Assemblers Suspend Southeast Asia Shipments After US Export Controls Take Effect, Sending Shares of Malaysian Data Center Operators Down Nearly 20 Per Cent

At least two of Nvidia's contracted server assemblers halted shipments to clients in Southeast Asia following the introduction of US chip export licensing requirements on 19 November, according to Nikkei Asia. Malaysian listed data center operators MN Holdings and Critical Holdings both fell nearly 20 per cent on the announcement, while Singapore moved to defend its status as a trusted technology hub and Malaysia's trade minister said companies applying to build data centers would need to disclose operators, sites and planned GPU imports.

Source: Nikkei Asia  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

President Trump Threatens a Tremendous Tax on US Companies That Build AI Data Centers Outside the United States as Alphabet and Amazon Shares Fall on Investor Concern Over the Policy Implications

President Donald Trump threatened on 20 November to impose what he called a tremendous tax on US companies including Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft that build AI data centers outside the United States, saying they could do so but would face the levy if they did. No rate, legal mechanism or executive order accompanied the statement as of 21 November, though Alphabet and Amazon shares each fell 3 to 4 per cent on the news, coming less than three weeks after voter concern over data center development contributed to Republican losses in the midterm elections.

Source: CNBC  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Turner & Townsend Finds US Data Center Construction Costs Rose 13 Per Cent in 2026, More Than Double the Global Rate and Triple the Decade Average, as Demand for AI Workloads Outpaces Powered Land and Skilled Labour

A Turner & Townsend report examining more than 500 projects across 62 global markets found that US data center construction costs increased 13 per cent year on year in 2026, more than twice the global rate and roughly triple the decade long average, driven by powered land shortages, competition for skilled labour and contractors pricing in greater risk on aggressive build schedules. The firm's North America lead said cost growth was expected to moderate to between 6 and 10 per cent in the coming year, while noting that AI specific facilities cost around 10 per cent more than traditional data centers in the US market.

Source: CNBC  ·  20 November 2026

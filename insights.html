Market Intelligence

Microsoft Commits More Than $10 Billion to AI and Cloud Infrastructure Across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait Through 2030, Pledging a Further $400 Million for Regional Subsea and Terrestrial Connectivity

Microsoft announced on 23 September 2026 that it plans to invest more than $10 billion in capital and operating expenditure in data center and AI infrastructure across four Gulf states through 2030, with its vice chair Brad Smith stating that the company would increase rather than scale back its regional commitments despite the ongoing Iran conflict. The total incorporates a previously announced $7.9 billion UAE pledge, with new spending in Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait extending Microsoft's Gulf footprint beyond the Emirates for the first time at this scale.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  23 September 2026
 New Builds

Saudi Arabia's SDAIA Breaks Ground on the Hexagon Data Centre in Riyadh, a 480MW Tier IV Facility Representing a $2.7 Billion Investment That Is Set to Become the Largest Dedicated Government Data Center in the World

The Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority began construction on the Hexagon Data Centre in Riyadh in January 2026, a 480 megawatt Tier IV campus designed to host cloud services for 193 government entities under the Kingdom's DEEM sovereign cloud programme. The facility, which will process all government data within the Kingdom's borders, forms a central pillar of Saudi Vision 2030's digital infrastructure strategy and is designed to meet the highest international standards for availability and resilience.

Source: Gulf News  ·  January 2026
 New Builds

French AI Infrastructure Group Sesterce Announces a Data Center Campus Costing More Than €10 Billion at the Former Kaipola Paper Mill in Jämsä, Central Finland, With a 200MW First Phase Already Under Construction and Long Term Potential Exceeding 1GW

Sesterce announced on 8 October 2026 that it would develop an AI data center campus on a former industrial site in Jämsä, with early groundworks already under way and capacity planned to grow from 200MW in the first phase to 600MW and ultimately more than 1GW over time. Chief executive Youssef El Manssouri confirmed to Reuters that the company has secured an anchor customer from one of the world's two leading AI laboratories, though neither the customer nor the financing parties have been named publicly.

Source: Reuters  ·  8 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Google Wins Planning Permission From An Coimisiún Pleanála for a Third Data Center Building at Its Grange Castle Campus in South Dublin After the Regulator Overturned a 2024 Council Refusal Over Grid Capacity Concerns

An Coimisiún Pleanála granted planning consent to Google Ireland on 28 September 2026 for a third data center building of 72,400 square metres at the Grange Castle Business Park, overturning a South Dublin County Council refusal from 2024 on the grounds that Google had already secured a grid connection from EirGrid in 2021 prior to the Council's decision. Conditions attached to the consent require Google to enter power purchase agreements before development commences and to use renewable fuel in its backup generators.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  28 September 2026
 Capex Watch

Amazon Data Services Malaysia Signs an Agreement to Acquire 18.4 Hectares of Freehold Land in Selangor From Sunsuria City for RM317 Million, Signalling Plans to Expand Its Data Center Presence in Malaysia Beyond the Existing Cyberjaya Campus

Sunsuria City Sdn Bhd disclosed on 29 September 2026 that it had agreed to sell approximately 18.4 hectares of vacant freehold land in the Mukim of Dengkil, around 40 kilometres south of Kuala Lumpur, to Amazon Data Services Malaysia for RM316.84 million, with completion expected in the second quarter of 2027 subject to state consent and shareholder approval. Amazon described the intended use as data processing and infrastructure services but has not disclosed a specific project or capacity target for the site.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  29 September 2026
 Market Intelligence

Singapore Based DayOne Data Centers Files a Form F-1 With the US Securities and Exchange Commission for a Proposed IPO on Nasdaq Under the Ticker DODC, With Revenue More Than Tripling Year on Year to $512 Million in the First Half of 2026

DayOne Data Centers, the Asia Pacific and European operator spun off from GDS Holdings in January 2025, filed a registration statement with the SEC on 6 October 2026 for an IPO on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker DODC, with Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan, BofA Securities and Citigroup acting as underwriters. The company reported revenue of $512 million for the six months to 30 June 2026, up from $151.5 million a year earlier, and intends to use the proceeds to fund construction of new data center projects across its APAC and European portfolio.

Source: Light Reading  ·  6 October 2026
 Capex Watch

TeraWulf Doubles Contracted Power Capacity at Its Muskie Data Campus in Eastern Kentucky From 500MW to 1GW After Amending Its Electric Service Agreement With Kentucky Power, Bringing the Second 500MW Phase Forward From 2030 to 2029

TeraWulf announced on 5 October 2026 that its subsidiary had signed an amended and restated electric service agreement with Kentucky Power, an American Electric Power company, increasing contracted capacity at the Muskie campus in Grayson County to 1 gigawatt and advancing the second phase delivery target by one year to 2029, subject to Kentucky Public Service Commission approval. The company said it continues to evaluate the potential to expand the campus to as much as 2GW over time, with the first 500MW phase remaining on course to begin ramping in the second half of 2028.

Source: 247 Wall St  ·  5 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

A Coalition Including Blackstone Backed QTS, NextEra Energy and Siemens Energy Forms to Counter Community Opposition to AI Data Center Developments Across Texas, Engaging Local Officials and Residents on Employment and Economic Benefits

Bloomberg reported on 1 October 2026 that an alliance of QTS Data Centers, NextEra Energy and Siemens Energy had formed to push back against grassroots resistance to data center projects in Texas, where community concerns about power demand, water consumption and visual impact have led to organised opposition to new developments. The coalition's president said its strategy focuses on engaging residents and local officials directly to communicate the scale of employment and economic activity generated by hyperscale AI infrastructure investment.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  1 October 2026

Market Intelligence

Microsoft Confirms Its Saudi Arabia East Azure Data Center Region Will Open to Customers in November 2026, Comprising Three Availability Zones in the Eastern Province and Offering Local Data Residency, Enterprise Security and Low Latency Access to Cloud and AI Services for the First Time Within the Kingdom

Microsoft announced in August 2026 that its Saudi Arabia East region, comprising three Azure Availability Zones in the Eastern Province, will become available to customers in November 2026, providing government agencies and enterprises with local data residency and low latency connectivity to cloud and AI services for the first time in the country. An IDC study commissioned by Microsoft estimates the region will generate $44 billion in new economic activity for the Saudi economy between 2027 and 2030, with the data center infrastructure supporting mission critical workloads in line with Saudi Vision 2030.

 Capex Watch

Microsoft Commits More Than $10 Billion Across Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait and Qatar Through 2030, Including $2 Billion in Fresh Commitments to Extend Its Gulf Cloud and AI Data Center Footprint Into Kuwait and Qatar Alongside Its Existing $7.9 Billion UAE Investment

Microsoft announced on 23 September 2026 that it would invest more than $10 billion across four Gulf states over the following four years, adding approximately $2 billion in new commitments to Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Qatar on top of the $7.9 billion pledge it had already made for the UAE. The announcement, timed to align with United Nations General Assembly side events, forms part of the company's strategy to extend sovereign AI computing capacity and local cloud infrastructure throughout the wider Gulf region.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  23 September 2026
 Capex Watch

UK Data Center Operator Virtus Secures a £2.45 Billion Green Financing Package from a Consortium of 13 Banks to Fund Its Saunderton Campus in Buckinghamshire, Investment at LONDON19 in Slough and Continued Expansion Across Continental Europe

Virtus Data Centres completed one of the largest UK bank financings ever arranged for a data center operator, closing a £2.45 billion package structured through green capital expenditure facilities with BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole CIB, Société Générale and Standard Chartered acting as lead coordinators and arrangers. Proceeds will support the Saunderton campus in Buckinghamshire, further investment at the LONDON19 site in Slough and the company's growth programme across continental Europe.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  24 September 2026
 Market Intelligence

Global Switch Selects Its Fully Renewable Energy Powered Paris Campus to Host One of the Biggest Nvidia Blackwell Deployments in Europe, With a Large Liquid Cooled Cluster Installed at Its DGX Ready Facility and Waste Heat to Be Supplied to the Neighbouring Municipality of Levallois Perret From 2027

Global Switch announced on 29 September 2026 that an undisclosed client had selected its Paris data center to host a large, predominantly liquid cooled Nvidia Blackwell cluster, which the company described as one of the biggest Nvidia Blackwell deployments in Europe and which delivers a fivefold improvement in inference performance relative to prior generation hardware. The Paris campus is powered entirely by renewable energy and is set to begin supplying waste heat to the municipality of Levallois Perret in 2027 under an existing heat offtake agreement, with a further agreement for the city of Clichy to follow.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  29 September 2026
 New Builds

New Australian Developer HyperDC Lodges a Planning Application With the Victorian Government for a $6 Billion, 400MW Data Center Campus on 39 Hectares at Moorabool, 75 Kilometres West of Melbourne, Adjacent to the 300MW Victorian Big Battery and AusNet Services' Moorabool Terminal Station

HyperDC Pty Ltd filed a permit application on 29 September 2026 for its MBL1 campus on 39 hectares at 715 Ballan Road in Moorabool, targeting 400MW of IT capacity in what would be the company's first development if planning consent is granted. The developer, led by former Villawood Properties acquisitions manager Gerard Adamsons, cited proximity to established high voltage power infrastructure and the adjacent Victorian Big Battery as strategic advantages of the selected site.

 Market Intelligence

Thailand's Data Center Business Policy Committee Approves a Standalone Legal Classification for Data Center Buildings and Proposes Five Size Categories Based on Power Consumption, With Facilities Above 100MW Expected to Be Designated as Hyperscale and Required to Locate Within Designated Industrial Estates

Thailand's national Data Center Business Policy Committee voted on 1 October 2026 to adopt a standalone legal definition for data center buildings, separating them from the existing large buildings category, and agreed in principle to introduce five size tiers defined primarily by electricity consumption. The proposed regulatory framework, targeting finalisation by mid October 2026, would require data centers above the hyperscale threshold of 100MW to operate within designated industrial estates and obtain licensing from the Energy Regulatory Commission.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  1 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Applied Digital Achieves Ready for Service Status on the Second Phase of Building 2 at Polaris Forge 1 in Ellendale, North Dakota, Adding 75MW Across Three Data Halls to Bring Total Operational Capacity at the Fully Leased CoreWeave Campus to 250MW

Applied Digital announced on 2 October 2026 that it had completed the second phase of Building 2 at its Polaris Forge 1 campus in Ellendale, activating 75MW of critical IT load across three 25MW data halls and bringing the entire 150MW building to full operational status. Combined with the earlier 100MW Building 1, total live capacity at the fully leased campus now stands at 250MW, with the site targeting 400MW at full build out.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  2 October 2026
 Capex Watch

AI Infrastructure Company Nebius Signs a 12 Year, 50MW Colocation Agreement With AIB Data Centers at the CLT1 Facility in South Carolina, With Two Data Halls to Be Delivered Within 10 and 14 Months and Customer Prepayments Set to Fund a Substantial Share of Development Costs

AIB Data Centers announced on 30 September 2026 that Nebius had signed a binding 12 year lease for 50MW of critical IT capacity at its CLT1 facility in South Carolina, with two five year renewal options and a structure in which customer prepayments under the initial term are expected to fund a substantial portion of build costs alongside project level debt and preferred equity. The agreement reflects a growing trend in which long term anchor tenant commitments are used to reduce the corporate equity required to finance new data center development.

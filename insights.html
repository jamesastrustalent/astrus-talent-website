Market Intelligence

Oracle Accelerates Data Center Expansion Across the UAE and Saudi Arabia as AI Demand Surges, Declining to Publicise New Site Locations After Iranian Attacks on Gulf Infrastructure

Oracle told The National at Oracle AI World in Las Vegas on 29 October 2026 that it had opened several new data center sites across the UAE and Saudi Arabia over the preceding six months and planned to add further capacity within the next six months to meet what it described as huge demand from cloud and AI customers. The company said it would not publicly disclose the locations of its new facilities following attacks on data centers in the Gulf region, including sites in the UAE and Bahrain, since the outbreak of conflict with Iran earlier in 2026.

Source: The National  ·  29 October 2026
 New Builds

Microsoft Confirms Its Saudi Arabia East Azure Region Will Open to Customers in November 2026 Across Three Availability Zones, Enabling Local Data Residency Ahead of AWS Saudi Launch in December

Microsoft announced that its Saudi Arabia East datacenter region, located in the Eastern Province of the Kingdom, would be available to customers in November 2026 across three Azure Availability Zones, supporting data residency requirements for Saudi government and private sector organisations. The region is part of a broader Gulf expansion by hyperscale cloud providers, with Amazon Web Services also committing a $5.3 billion Saudi cloud region for launch in December 2026 as both companies respond to strong enterprise and government demand for sovereign cloud services in the Kingdom.

Source: Microsoft News  ·  August 2026
 Market Intelligence

Greece Publishes Draft Legislation Requiring New Data Centers to Source 60% of Annual Electricity From Renewables and Establishes a National Registry Under the Country's Energy Regulator

The Greek Ministry of Environment and Energy released draft data center legislation for public consultation on 24 October 2026 that would require new facilities to cover at least 60% of their annual electricity needs from renewable sources, with the energy regulator RAAEY tasked with establishing a national data center registry to improve transparency across the sector. Projects exceeding 200 MW would receive priority treatment in grid connection applications, and operators would be required to cover the full costs of connection and reinforcement works, in line with regulatory reforms being introduced across several European markets.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Finland's Planned and Active Data Center Investment Pipeline Surpasses €67 Billion Making It One of Europe's Fastest Growing Digital Infrastructure Destinations, With Google, Microsoft and TikTok Among the Largest Investors

Data published by the Confederation of Finnish Industries in October 2026 shows that the total value of planned and committed data center investments in Finland has exceeded €67 billion, equivalent to approximately $75 billion, earning the country comparisons to the Texas of Europe for digital infrastructure. Google alone has committed more than €13 billion to Finnish AI data centers in its largest European push to date, Cerebras has begun constructing a 165 MW facility in Mikkeli, and Sesterce is investing over €10 billion on a former paper mill campus, with Microsoft and TikTok also holding significant commitments in the country.

Source: Data Centre Magazine  ·  October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Malaysia Sets a 30% Minimum Renewable Energy Requirement for New Data Centers and Approves 5 GW of Additional Capacity Conditional on Operators Meeting the Green Energy Rule

Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof announced on 27 October 2026 that Malaysia would require all new data centers to source at least 30% of their electricity from renewable energy, with the Energy Commission tasked with developing the implementation framework covering onsite generation and corporate renewable energy supply schemes. The government simultaneously approved a further 5 GW of data center capacity, conditional on compliance with the renewables rule, as Malaysia seeks to balance a rapidly expanding industry with its sustainability commitments ahead of 2030.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  27 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Asia Pacific's Data Center Development Pipeline Reaches a Record 26.5 GW in H1 2026 as Electricity Availability Emerges as the Principal Constraint on Growth Across the Region's Major Markets

Cushman & Wakefield's H1 2026 Asia Pacific data center update shows that the region's development pipeline expanded by approximately 7.1 GW in six months to reach a record 26.5 GW, as AI workload demand continues to outpace the speed at which new supply can be brought online. Electricity availability has emerged as the primary bottleneck, with CBRE also noting in its regional outlook that land and power constraints remain acute across markets including Singapore, Tokyo and Sydney, and that oversupply is not a near term risk anywhere in the region.

Source: Cushman & Wakefield  ·  H1 2026
 Market Intelligence

Oregon Governor Tina Kotek Orders the State Environmental Regulator to Pause Air and Water Pollution Permits for New and Expanding Data Centers Until July 2027, Blocking Most Hyperscale Development in the State

Governor Tina Kotek directed the Oregon Department of Environmental Quality on 9 October 2026 to suspend the processing and issue of air and water discharge permits for new or expanding data center facilities until 1 July 2027, widening a September directive that had already paused state land sales for data center projects. Industry observers described the order as a de facto statewide moratorium, noting that hyperscale operators cannot legally build large data centers in Oregon without the pollution permits the directive freezes, with Kotek signalling plans to bring formal moratorium legislation before the 2027 session.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  October 2026
 New Builds

Google Is Confirmed as the Developer Behind a Proposed 1,200 MW Data Center Campus Across Approximately 1,000 Acres in Goochland County, Virginia, One of the Largest Single Campus Plans in the United States

Goochland County announced on 28 October 2026 that Google was the company behind a proposed 1,200 MW data center campus covering around 1,000 acres at the West Creek Business Park, after the project first surfaced in a State Corporation Commission engineering filing in July. Google confirmed the connection following the county's public release, and the site adds to a growing Richmond area footprint that includes a campus already under construction in Chesterfield County, as Virginia continues to attract a disproportionately large share of US data center investment.

Source: Richmond BizSense  ·  28 October 2026

Market Intelligence

S&P Global Says the UAE and Saudi Arabia Will Account for More Than 80 Percent of New Middle East Data Center Capacity Through 2030, Forecasting a Combined Addition of up to 3GW of IT Power as Sovereign AI Programmes Drive a Sharp Acceleration in Regional Infrastructure Investment

An S&P Global Ratings report published on 5 October 2026 identified the UAE and Saudi Arabia as the dominant forces in Middle East data center expansion, together expected to represent more than 80 percent of new regional capacity through to 2030 at estimated build costs of approximately $11 to $13 million per megawatt. The agency pointed to hyperscaler demand, sovereign AI strategies and supportive government investment frameworks in both countries as the primary drivers of growth, with Saudi programmes including HUMAIN targeting up to 1.9GW of capacity by 2030.

Source: Khaleej Times  ·  5 October 2026
 New Builds

Dammam Based Construction Firm Al Yamama Wins the Civil Infrastructure Contract for HUMAIN's 6GW AI Data Center Campus in Riyadh, Covering Power Distribution at Three Voltage Levels, Two Major Substations and a 2,000 MVA Bulk Supply Point Across a 24 Square Kilometre Site in East Riyadh

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund backed AI company HUMAIN has awarded the site infrastructure contract for its 6GW Riyadh data center campus to Al Yamama, with the development planned across six plots each targeting 1GW of capacity in the Al Saad district of east Riyadh. The contract scope covers high voltage power distribution at 380kV, 132kV and 33kV, two large substations, stormwater drainage, a sewage treatment plant, roads and underground fibre, and will be delivered via an early contractor involvement model.

Source: Enterprise AM  ·  27 September 2026
 New Builds

atNorth Announces a New 75MW Data Center Campus in Salo, Finland With a Pathway to 230MW and an Expected Developer Investment of Two Billion Euros, Following the Completion of a $4 Billion Acquisition of the Company by CPP Investments and Equinix in September 2026

atNorth said on 5 October 2026 that it will develop FIN05, a new data center on a 28.6 hectare site in Salo connected to a Fingrid substation, with first power targeted for the third quarter of 2028 and a pathway to 160MW of IT load and 230MW of total capacity. The announcement followed the completion of CPP Investments and Equinix's $4 billion acquisition of atNorth, and the company said customer owned hardware and its installation could represent a further investment of up to ten billion euros beyond its own development spend.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  5 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Data Center Opposition Spreads From the United States to Europe and Asia With STL Partners Estimating $42 Billion of European Projects Stalled or Cancelled, Thailand Having Frozen 49 Active Builds and Multiple Governments Moving to Tighten Regulatory Frameworks

CNBC reported on 3 October 2026 that community and regulatory opposition to data center development is intensifying well beyond the United States, with Thailand freezing 49 active builds in September 2026 and 22 of 25 Seoul district mayors requesting a review of sites planned in residential areas, while STL Partners estimated approximately $42 billion of European data center investment had been stalled or cancelled. Denmark, Scotland and Spain have each moved to restrict development through emergency legislation, grid access rules and mandatory renewable energy sourcing requirements respectively.

Source: CNBC  ·  3 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Jardine Matheson's Chief Executive Tells the Milken Asia Summit That Political Risk Is His Biggest Concern for the Asia Pacific Data Center Boom Given the Region's Dependence on a Small Number of US Technology Companies as the Primary Source of Infrastructure Demand

Bloomberg reported on 9 October 2026 that Jardine Matheson's chief executive named political risk as the factor he thinks about most in relation to the Asia Pacific data center market, citing the degree to which regional infrastructure investment depends on sustained capital commitment from a small number of US hyperscalers. The warning came as US technology export controls and trade policy have emerged as a material variable for data center demand forecasts across both South East and North East Asia.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  9 October 2026
 Capex Watch

Singapore's DayOne Data Centers Files an F-1 Prospectus With the SEC for a Nasdaq IPO Under Ticker DODC, Targeting a Valuation of up to $20 Billion as the Asia Pacific Operator Reports $512 Million in Revenue and 2.3GW of Contracted Bookings for the First Half of 2026

DayOne, which was spun out of China's GDS Holdings in January 2025 and operates or develops more than 500MW of data center capacity across Asia Pacific plus sites in Finland and Spain, filed its registration statement with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on 6 October 2026 with Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan, BofA Securities and Citigroup as joint underwriters. The company reported revenue of $512 million and a net loss of $81.9 million for the first half of 2026 and has secured approximately 2.3GW in contracted bookings, though the offering size and price range have not yet been determined.

Source: The Star  ·  8 October 2026
 New Builds

Planning Applications Are Filed for an Anthropic Linked $366 Million Data Center Project in Cedar Creek, Bastrop County, Texas, Adding to the Large Scale AI Infrastructure Footprint Anthropic Is Assembling Across Central Texas Through Leases and Development Partnerships

Data Center Dynamics reported on 9 October 2026 that planning applications had been filed for a $366 million data center project in Cedar Creek, Bastrop County, linked to Anthropic, the latest in a series of Texas infrastructure commitments the AI company has been accumulating throughout 2026. The Cedar Creek filing follows Anthropic's previously reported $9.1 billion campus lease in Rockdale and a further site near Waco, as the company scales its physical compute base to support growing inference demand for its Claude models.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 October 2026
 New Builds

Valar Atomics Applies to Build a 9.4GW Nuclear Powered Data Center Campus on Federal Land in Utah Using 456 Small Modular Reactors Under a Plan Called Project Beehive, With Non Nuclear Site Work Targeted to Begin Before the End of 2026 and First Reactors Expected Online in 2028

Valar Atomics filed a proposal with the Bureau of Land Management for a 9,000 acre campus near Price in Carbon County, Utah, combining 456 small reactors, a fuel plant and large scale data center facilities designed to generate approximately 9.4GW, with non nuclear construction targeted to begin before the close of 2026 and full site operation planned for 2032. The company's Ward 250 test reactor in Emery County has already reached criticality, which the US Department of Energy described as the first DOE authorised reactor constructed outside a national laboratory.

Source: NPR  ·  30 September 2026

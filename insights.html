Market Intelligence

Iran Linked Vessels Tracked Circling Subsea Internet Cables in the Gulf of Oman After Tehran Issues Threats to Attack Digital Infrastructure Including Data Centers

Vessels tracked by maritime intelligence company Windward have been spotted taking unusual routes over subsea cables passing through the Gulf of Oman after Iran threatened to attack undersea cables and data centers if the United States and Israel launched new strikes. At least four Iran linked ships, including sanctioned tankers, were observed near cable routes with some disabling their AIS transponders, raising concern for the seven subsea cables running through the Strait of Hormuz, though no damage to cables has been confirmed.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  6 November 2026
 New Builds

A Mubbadrah Led Consortium Plans a 400 Megawatt Hyperscale AI Data Center in Oman Backed by 3 Billion Dollars of Investment and 400 Megawatts of Dedicated Solar and Battery Storage

Mubbadrah Investment has announced a consortium plan to develop up to 400 megawatts of AI data center capacity in Oman, backed by approximately 3 billion dollars in initial investment, with dedicated solar generation and battery energy storage to be developed by O2 Global Energy. US based AI infrastructure company Crusoe has indicated interest as a potential off-taker for capacity from the facility, which forms part of a broader surge in hyperscale investment positioning Oman alongside the UAE and Saudi Arabia as a primary Gulf compute destination.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  11 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Activists Break Into Microsoft's Amsterdam Construction Site as the Stop Dirty Data Centres Campaign Draws More Than 2,000 Marchers in Dublin and 1,300 in Helsinki Across Two Days of European Action

Up to 200 activists broke through the security fence of a Microsoft data center construction site in Amsterdam on 6 November 2026 as part of a two day coordinated European campaign involving more than 100 groups calling for a freeze on new data center development. The Stop Dirty Data Centres campaign staged marches in Dublin, Helsinki and Milan across 6 to 10 November, with organisers citing environmental concerns about water and power consumption, and the protests have drawn fresh attention to planning frameworks across Ireland, the Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Croatia, Denmark, Finland and France.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  6 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

The European Central Bank Is Weighing a Targeted Review of Eurozone Bank Lending to Data Center Projects, Examining Cash Flow Modelling and Collateral Valuations as Supervisory Concern Grows

Senior European Central Bank officials have internally discussed and consulted with supervised institutions on the possibility of a focused review of data center lending, prompted by concern that rapid AI infrastructure expansion could leave banks exposed if valuations soften or community opposition to new builds intensifies. Any review is expected to be narrower in scope than earlier ECB exercises but would involve on site inspections examining how banks project future cash flows, value collateral and provision for defaults, with no formal approach yet announced.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  6 November 2026
 New Builds

Equinix Opens the 290 Million Dollar SG6 Data Center in Singapore Four Months Ahead of Schedule, Delivering 8 Megawatts of AI Ready Capacity Under Singapore's Post Moratorium Framework

Equinix has opened SG6 in Singapore ahead of its original first quarter 2027 schedule, delivering 8 megawatts of IT capacity and approximately 1,550 cabinets on an initial investment of roughly 290 million US dollars, with the facility designed to reach 20 megawatts at full build out. SG6 was developed under Singapore's Data Centre Call for Application framework, supports liquid cooling for power intensive GPU deployments, and includes plans for a joint innovation facility with the National University of Singapore targeted for launch in 2027.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  6 November 2026

SoftBank, Grab and PETROS Sign a Joint Development Agreement for AI Data Centers in Sarawak With 1 Billion Dollars in Seed Capital and an Ambition to Build More Than 5 Gigawatts of Compute Across Malaysia

SoftBank, Grab and PETROS signed a joint development agreement on 9 November 2026 to build AI data center infrastructure in Sarawak, Malaysia, with initial seed capital of approximately 1 billion US dollars and SoftBank contributing 800 million dollars alongside its partners. The Sarawak commitment forms part of a broader ambition to eventually develop more than 5 gigawatts of AI compute capacity across Malaysia, as the country attracts significant hyperscale capital despite its total installed data center power capacity of only around 1.3 gigawatts as of mid-2026.

Source: The Edge Malaysia  ·  9 November 2026
 New Builds

Google Commits 17 Billion Dollars to Its First Wyoming Data Center Campus Near Cheyenne, Powered by 702 Megawatts of Natural Gas Fuel Cells Paired With Carbon Capture and Underground Storage

Google confirmed on 5 November 2026 that it will build a data center campus in Laramie County near Cheyenne, committing 17 billion dollars and partnering with AEP, Tallgrass Energy, Black Hills Energy and Bloom Energy on a 702 megawatt natural gas fuel cell plant designed to capture and store the majority of its CO2 emissions underground at Tallgrass Energy's Eastern Wyoming Sequestration Hub. The announcement comes as hyperscale operators turn to on site generation to bypass grid connection queues that cannot accommodate AI infrastructure deployment timelines, while framing the project as a lower carbon alternative to conventional gas power.

Source: Google Blog  ·  5 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

BloombergNEF Finds That On Site Gas Plants Proposed for US Data Centers Would Emit Around 345 Million Metric Tonnes of CO2 Each Year, Adding Approximately 10 Per Cent to 2025 US Power Sector Emissions

A BloombergNEF report commissioned by Bloomberg Philanthropies and published on 11 November 2026 found that the 165 gigawatts of on site gas generation proposed alongside US data centers would emit approximately 345 million metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent annually, representing roughly 10 per cent of total 2025 US power sector emissions. Data center developers are turning to on site generation because grid connection queues cannot accommodate AI infrastructure deployment timelines, with more than 90 per cent of the 181 gigawatts of announced on site gas capacity globally concentrated in the United States.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  11 November 2026

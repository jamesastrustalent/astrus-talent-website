Market Intelligence

Trump's Threatened Overseas Data Center Tax Raises Direct Concern for Gulf Sovereign AI Programmes as Saudi Arabia and Abu Dhabi Both Depend on US Technology Partnerships and Chip Supply Agreements That Could Be Caught by the Measure

Forbes reported on 21 November that President Trump's threatened tax on US companies building data centers outside the United States has created uncertainty for the Gulf's largest AI projects, including the AWS HUMAIN zone in Saudi Arabia and the Stargate UAE campus in Abu Dhabi, both of which were agreed under close alignment with US technology firms. Analysts said the administration's position creates a tension between encouraging Gulf investment in US AI partnerships and penalising the same companies for building the capacity those partnerships require, as the UAE still needs US government authorisation to receive advanced Nvidia chips under existing export controls.

Source: Forbes  ·  21 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Oracle Expands Its Data Center Presence in UAE and Saudi Arabia as AI Demand Pushes Projections Higher, With S&P Forecasting That UAE Data Center Power Use Could More Than Double by 2030

The National reported on 20 November that Oracle is opening additional data centers in the UAE and Saudi Arabia to meet rising AI workload demand, with Abu Dhabi's 5GW AI campus continuing construction and the emirate developing a dedicated tariff structure for AI data facilities. S&P Global projected that data center electricity use in the UAE could more than double by 2030, though some analysts cautioned that the current pace of AI driven demand may moderate once hyperscalers complete their initial build cycles.

Source: The National  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Spain Approves Royal Decree Requiring New Data Centers to Source at Least 80 Per Cent of Electricity From Newly Installed Renewables, With Industry Body Warning That Up to 90 Per Cent of €67 Billion in Planned Investment Could Relocate

Spain's Council of Ministers approved a royal decree on 21 November setting rules for new data centers to cover at least 80 per cent of their electricity consumption from renewable capacity commissioned no more than 18 months before operations begin, including an hourly matching requirement during operation. Industry association Spain DC warned that up to 90 per cent of the €67 billion committed to data center investment in Spain for 2026 to 2030 was at risk of relocating to countries with less restrictive rules, while the government said the requirements were necessary to control the environmental impact of AI infrastructure growth ahead of the November general election.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  21 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

ENGIE and Google Sign an Energy Partnership Agreement for a Planned 500MW Data Center Near Châteauroux in France, With Discussions Under Way on Routing Recovered Waste Heat Free of Charge to Local District Heating Networks

ENGIE and Google signed a memorandum of understanding on 13 November under which ENGIE would become Google's preferred energy partner for a planned data center of up to 500MW at Étrechet, near Châteauroux in the Indre region, which would be the company's first facility built on its own account in France. The parties are also assessing whether waste heat recovered from the facility could be distributed free of charge to local district heating networks, though both the energy supply contract and the heat arrangements remain subject to further agreements and are not yet finalised.

Source: IT Pro  ·  13 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

SoftBank, Grab and Petroleum Sarawak Announce US$1.85 Billion in Initial Capital for AI Data Center Infrastructure Across Malaysia, Targeting a Long Term Programme of More Than Five Gigawatts of Capacity

SoftBank Group, Grab Holdings and Petroleum Sarawak Berhad announced on 9 November a combined US$1.85 billion in initial seed capital to develop AI data center and compute infrastructure in Sarawak and Peninsular Malaysia, with SoftBank contributing US$1 billion, Grab US$750 million and PETROS US$100 million. The investment is structured as conditional staged funding tied to development and customer contract milestones, and the partners said they would seek additional investors and hyperscaler customers to support expansion towards a targeted programme of five gigawatts or more of capacity.

Source: Malay Mail  ·  10 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

NextDC Chief Executive Tells Investors That Uncontracted Australian Data Center Projects Will Remain Paused Until Community Trust Is Won, After Power Upgrade Works at the Melbourne M3 Campus Were Suspended Over Local Opposition

NextDC chief executive Craig Scroggie told the UBS Australasia conference on 9 November that projects without customer contracts would remain on hold until the company had earned community support, following a halt to electricity network upgrade works at its M3 Melbourne campus by network operator Jemena amid local opposition. The pause, announced in October after NextDC cut its FY27 revenue guidance by approximately A$140 million, was accompanied by a 42 per cent fall in the company's share price, with Goodman Group and Digital Realty subsequently acquiring a combined 9.9 per cent stake for A$715 million.

Source: Reuters  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

SpaceX Files Plans for a US$24 Billion AI Supercomputing Data Center and Natural Gas Power Plant at Its Starbase Site in South Texas, Circumventing Local Data Center Moratoriums in Nearby Brownsville and Harlingen

SpaceX filed regulatory notices in November for a natural gas power plant and AI supercomputing data center on a 444-acre site within the city of Starbase in Cameron County, South Texas, with initial phases of the project estimated at approximately US$24 billion. The Texas Tribune reported that the development falls within a 45-year development agreement covering Starbase that bars local moratoriums, allowing SpaceX to proceed despite restriction measures adopted by nearby Brownsville and Harlingen, while Cameron County commissioners declined to hold a public hearing on the company's request for a 10-year property tax abatement.

Source: Texas Tribune  ·  10 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Nearly 40 Per Cent of Planned US Data Center Capacity Sits in States Where Newly Elected Governors Have Vowed to Restrict Construction, With Voters Approving 27 of 41 Ballot Measures Targeting the Industry Across 10 States

A BloombergNEF analysis reported by Insurance Journal on 6 November found that approximately 40 per cent of pending US data center capacity is planned in states where governors elected in the November midterm elections had pledged to restrict or pause development, with Democratic-controlled states expected to pursue utility and permitting policies that slow construction timelines. Voters approved 27 of the 41 data center restriction measures on the ballot across 10 states, including nearly all of a large package of such measures in Ohio, adding further political pressure to an already stretched pipeline of planned facilities.

Source: Insurance Journal  ·  6 November 2026

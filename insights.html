Market Intelligence

Humain and Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund Sign SAR 2.5 Billion in Financing Agreements on 8 November to Fund GPU Procurement and Grid Infrastructure for the Kingdom's AI Data Center Programme

Humain and the National Infrastructure Fund (Infra) signed two financing agreements totalling SAR 2.5 billion ($667 million) on 8 November 2026, marking the first transactions under Infra's AI Data Center Financing Programme. The first facility of up to SAR 1.1 billion funds the procurement and deployment of GPUs representing approximately 25 megawatts of IT load, while the second of up to SAR 1.4 billion covers substations, transmission lines and grid connections for Humain's Saudi data center sites, with Aramco having completed its 49 per cent acquisition of Humain on 1 November and the company targeting 3 gigawatts of AI data center capacity by 2030.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  14 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Inaugural Data Center World Middle East Opens at the Dusit Thani Abu Dhabi on 17 November With UAE Ministry Officials and Industry Leaders Debating Power, Security and Architecture Priorities for the Country's AI Build Out

The two day event brought together representatives from the UAE Ministry of Investment, Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure, Khazna Data Centers, Core42, AWS, Google, Microsoft and Oracle to discuss the challenges of moving from announced gigawatts to energised megawatts as the region accelerates AI capacity deployment. Speakers highlighted that aligning grid connections, security frameworks and system architecture early in the development cycle is essential if the UAE's planned capacity is to come online on schedule, with Khazna currently building G42's 1 gigawatt Stargate UAE cluster in partnership with OpenAI, Oracle, NVIDIA, Cisco and SoftBank.

Source: Zawya  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Spain's Government Seeks Approval for a Ministerial Decree Requiring Data Centers to Source 80 Per Cent of Their Electricity From Renewables on an Hourly Basis Before the 29 November General Election

The proposed decree would be passed without parliamentary approval and require facilities to match 80 per cent of their power consumption with renewable energy on an hourly basis, with industry body SpainDC warning the measure would create the most restrictive data center framework in the European Union. CBRE estimates developers have announced 10.5 gigawatts of capacity across Spain and Portugal, of which only about 4.5 gigawatts is considered realistically deliverable given grid and permitting constraints, while the Aragón regional government called the draft a de facto prohibition on new construction.

Source: CNBC  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Deutsche Telekom Doubles Its Munich AI Factory to 20 Megawatts With NVIDIA B300 Blackwell Ultra GPUs After the Facility Sold Out Eight Months After Its February 2026 Opening

Deutsche Telekom announced on 9 November 2026 that it will expand its Industrial AI Cloud at Munich's Tucherpark from roughly 10 megawatts to 20 megawatts by adding NVIDIA B300 Blackwell Ultra GPUs alongside the B200 systems already in production, in a further investment of approximately €250 million. The facility, which opened in February 2026 as one of Europe's largest dedicated AI compute sites, was sold out ahead of the expansion announcement, and the company said it is exploring a further capacity increase in Bavaria subject to financing and economic viability.

Source: Deutsche Telekom  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Commerce Department Publishes Interim Rule Requiring Licences for Advanced AI Chip Exports to Data Centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia, Citing Risk of Diversion to Restricted Parties

An interim rule published on 16 November requires data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia to obtain a US government licence before importing advanced AI chips from Nvidia and other American suppliers, targeting facilities that supply compute capacity above a defined threshold to companies headquartered in China. Shares in Malaysian AI infrastructure group YTL Power fell nearly 10 per cent on the day of the announcement, and Singapore's government said it was engaging with US authorities to understand the scope and implementation of the new measures.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  16 November 2026
 New Builds

Equinix Opens SG6 as Singapore's First Data Center Built Under the City State's Updated Green Standards, Investing $290 Million in an 8 Megawatt AI Ready First Phase With Expansion to 20 Megawatts Planned

Equinix opened SG6, its sixth Singapore data center, on 6 November 2026 with an initial capacity of 8 megawatts and $290 million invested in the first phase, making it the first facility in the country built under Singapore's stricter sustainability requirements for new data center capacity. The site is designed for AI, high performance computing and cloud workloads and is planned to reach 20 megawatts at full build out, with a co innovation facility in partnership with the National University of Singapore due to open in 2027.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  6 November 2026

SpaceX Files Plans for a $24 Billion Data Center and Gas Fired Power Plant at Starbase on 444 Acres Near the Port of Brownsville, Sidestepping a 90 Day Development Moratorium Approved by Brownsville Days Earlier

SpaceX proposed the campus days after Brownsville's city commission unanimously approved a 90 day halt to data center development in the city, with the company having its preferred site removed from Brownsville's jurisdiction and annexed by the adjacent city of Starbase ahead of filing. The project forms part of a broader commitment of more than $74 billion in AI supercomputing campuses across Texas and Arizona, with the Texas site targeting more than 500 permanent jobs and a property tax abatement hearing scheduled for 9 December.

Source: Houston Chronicle  ·  11 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Voters Have Supported Limits on Data Center Development in 46 of the 47 Local Ballot Measures Decided in 2026, Including Outright Bans in California, Florida and Ohio, With Further Votes Due in December

Ballotpedia reported on 15 November 2026 that voters across the United States had decided 47 local ballot measures related to data center development during 2026, with 46 resulting in restrictions, moratoriums or outright bans, including five full bans approved in California, Florida and Ohio at November 3 elections. Analysts noted that the results will primarily affect projects planned from 2028 onwards, while developers in Arkansas, North Dakota and New Hampshire face further local votes in December.

Source: Ballotpedia  ·  15 November 2026

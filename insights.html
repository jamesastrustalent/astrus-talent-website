New Builds

AWS Sets December 2026 Launch for Saudi Arabia's First Cloud Region, Backed by $5.3 Billion Investment and an AI Zone Partnership with Humain

Amazon Web Services has confirmed its inaugural cloud infrastructure region in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia is on track to launch in December 2026, supported by more than $5.3 billion in planned infrastructure investment across three availability zones. In parallel, AWS and PIF owned AI firm Humain will make up to 50 megawatts of capacity available in Saudi Arabia's first dedicated AI Zone by 2028, incorporating AWS Trainium chips and Nvidia AI infrastructure for training and inference workloads, alongside a commitment to train 100,000 Saudi citizens for digital careers ahead of the region opening.

Source: Asharq Al-Awsat  ·  2 September 2026
 Market Intelligence

Accenture and AWS Sign Six Year Strategic Collaboration to Accelerate Cloud and AI Adoption Across the Middle East

Accenture has signed a six year strategic collaboration agreement with Amazon Web Services to help businesses and governments across the Middle East accelerate cloud migration and AI adoption, deploying a dedicated regional team of architects and delivery leads across priority sectors including energy, financial services and the public sector. The agreement provides dedicated Migration Acceleration Programme support to help clients modernise infrastructure and migrate critical workloads, as Gulf states continue to direct record investment into the AI infrastructure underpinning their national digital transformation agendas.

Source: ZAWYA  ·  1 September 2026
 New Builds

SoftBank Commits €75 Billion to Build 5GW of AI Data Center Capacity Across France in Its Largest Ever European Infrastructure Pledge

SoftBank Group announced at the Choose France summit a commitment to build 5 gigawatts of AI data center capacity across France, with a first phase of €45 billion to deliver 3.1 gigawatts in the Hauts-de-France region across sites at Dunkirk, Bosquel and Bouchain by 2031. The facilities will draw primarily on France's low carbon nuclear grid and will serve AI developers, cloud providers, enterprises, public institutions and research organisations across the continent, in SoftBank Group's single largest AI infrastructure commitment in Europe.

Source: SoftBank Group  ·  31 May 2026
 Market Intelligence

Grid Constraints Drive 63% of New European Data Center Capacity Beyond the Five Main Hub Markets as AI Demand Accelerates Decentralisation

In the second quarter of 2026, 63 per cent of Europe's newly added data center capacity was delivered outside the five established markets of Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris and Dublin, as power shortages and land constraints forced developers to seek grid ready sites in Spain, the Nordics and Central and Eastern Europe. CBRE data shows signings by AI focused providers in the Nordic region reached 420 megawatts in Q2, a 4.7-fold increase on the same period in 2025, with greenfield projects now averaging 175 kilometres from hub cities compared with 46 kilometres for campuses delivered between 2022 and 2025.

Source: The Next Web  ·  August 2026
 New Builds

CoreWeave Enters Asia Pacific with a 360MW Indonesia Data Center Plan Across Three Facilities, Its Largest Geographic Expansion Since Founding

AI cloud provider CoreWeave announced its first data center presence in the Asia Pacific region, with plans to build three facilities in Indonesia totalling 360 megawatts of contracted IT power, expected to come online in 2028 and targeted at AI labs, startups, enterprises and government customers across Southeast Asia. CoreWeave will own and operate all three sites and train a local team to manage them, marking the company's most significant geographic expansion beyond its established US and European base into one of the world's fastest growing digital economies.

Source: CoreWeave  ·  4 August 2026
 Capex Watch

NTT Data Plans $9 Billion Investment to Quadruple Japan Data Center Capacity to 1GW by 2033 as AI Demand Surges

NTT Data Group, Japan's largest data center operator, expects to spend at least $9 billion through 2033 to quadruple its computing capacity to 1 gigawatt, adding roughly 750 megawatts of new capacity across the country to serve soaring demand from organisations seeking AI capability, Bloomberg reports. Japan has emerged as the second largest operational data center market in Asia Pacific, with its total reaching 1.8 gigawatts in the first half of 2026, ahead of India and behind only mainland China, as the wider APAC development pipeline reached a record 26.5 gigawatts across the same period.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  3 August 2026
 Capex Watch

Crusoe Signs $13 Billion Five Year AI Cloud Deal with Jane Street and Raises $3 Billion at a $30 Billion Valuation

AI data center startup Crusoe has signed a five year cloud computing contract with quantitative trading firm Jane Street Group valued at approximately $13 billion, giving Jane Street access to clusters of GPUs and AI infrastructure through Crusoe's cloud platform, Bloomberg reports. The deal helped Crusoe close a $3 billion fundraising round at a $30 billion valuation, led jointly by Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners with participation from Mubadala Capital, cementing the company's position as a significant independent AI infrastructure provider alongside hyperscaler customers including Meta and Oracle.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  3 September 2026
 Market Intelligence

California Judge Orders Full Environmental Review of 330MW Imperial County Data Center Over Power, Water and Air Quality Risks

A California superior court judge has ruled that a proposed 330 megawatt data center in Imperial County must undergo a full environmental impact review before construction can proceed, citing the project's exceptional power demand, which could exceed the total electricity use of Imperial County in 2024, as well as risks from water supply, large scale battery storage and natural gas backup generators. The ruling reflects growing regulatory scrutiny of large scale data center proposals across the US, where court challenges, community opposition and grid constraints are increasingly shaping the pace and location of AI infrastructure investment.

Source: Data Center Knowledge  ·  1 September 2026

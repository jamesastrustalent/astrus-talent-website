Market Intelligence

Humain and Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund Sign SAR 2.5 Billion in Financing Agreements on 8 November to Fund GPU Procurement and Grid Infrastructure for the Kingdom's AI Data Center Programme

Humain and the National Infrastructure Fund (Infra) signed two financing agreements totalling SAR 2.5 billion ($667 million) on 8 November 2026, marking the first transactions under Infra's AI Data Center Financing Programme. The first facility of up to SAR 1.1 billion funds the procurement and deployment of GPUs representing approximately 25 megawatts of IT load, while the second of up to SAR 1.4 billion covers substations, transmission lines and grid connections for Humain's Saudi data center sites, with Aramco having completed its 49 per cent acquisition of Humain on 1 November and the company targeting 3 gigawatts of AI data center capacity by 2030.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  14 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Microsoft Opens Its Saudi Arabia East Cloud Region to Customers This Month, Adding Three Availability Zones in the Eastern Province as Part of a More Than $10 Billion Gulf Investment Commitment Through 2030

Microsoft's Saudi Arabia East datacenter region is now available to customers in November 2026, following construction completion in December 2024, allowing government and private sector organisations to host eligible cloud and AI workloads locally across three availability zones in the Eastern Province. The launch forms part of Microsoft's pledge to invest more than $10 billion in Gulf cloud and AI infrastructure by 2030, and comes as AWS prepares to open its own Saudi region in December, with Google and Oracle already operating national cloud regions in the Kingdom.

Source: Microsoft  ·  November 2026
 New Builds

Colt Data Centre Services Breaks Ground on a 54 Megawatt Two Building Campus East of Berlin on 10 November, With Berlin 1 Due Online by the End of 2028 as Part of a €2 Billion German Expansion Programme

Colt Data Centre Services started construction on its Berlin 1 data center on 10 November 2026, on a 9.5 acre site approximately 10 kilometres east of the capital, with electrical contractor H&MV Engineering delivering a 110 kilovolt substation for the facility. The two building campus will provide 54 megawatts of IT capacity upon completion and forms part of a €2 billion, four site German expansion programme first announced in April 2025.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  10 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Deutsche Telekom Doubles Its Munich AI Factory to 20 Megawatts With NVIDIA B300 Blackwell Ultra GPUs After the Facility Sold Out Eight Months After Its February 2026 Opening

Deutsche Telekom announced on 9 November 2026 that it will expand its Industrial AI Cloud at Munich's Tucherpark from roughly 10 megawatts to 20 megawatts by adding NVIDIA B300 Blackwell Ultra GPUs alongside the B200 systems already in production, in a further investment of approximately €250 million. The facility, which opened in February 2026 as one of Europe's largest dedicated AI compute sites, was sold out ahead of the expansion announcement, and the company said it is exploring a further capacity increase in Bavaria subject to financing and economic viability.

Source: Deutsche Telekom  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

New South Wales Data Center Inquiry Finds Planning Approvals Failed to Consider Cumulative Infrastructure Demands and Calls for Operators to Fund Their Own Grid and Water Connections

A parliamentary inquiry into data center development in New South Wales published its findings in November 2026, concluding that the state's fast tracked planning approvals regime failed to consider the cumulative effect of multiple large projects on shared grid, water and community infrastructure, and recommending that operators pay the full cost of the connections their facilities require and use more renewable energy and recycled water. The findings follow the cancellation of several large Sydney proposals, including Goodman Group's A$1.2 billion Lane Cove development, amid sustained community opposition to suburban sites.

Source: Reuters  ·  5 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Australia's NextDC Suspends All Uncontracted Data Center Projects After Power Delays and Community Opposition at Its Melbourne M3 Site Send the Company's Share Price Down 42 Per Cent

NextDC announced on 9 November 2026 that it will halt work on all data center projects without customer contracts in place, following the forced suspension of its Melbourne M3 expansion after power delays and local opposition, which triggered a forecast cut in October and a 42 per cent decline in its share price over the following weeks. CEO Craig Scroggie said construction cannot restart until the company has clearly communicated that the underlying problems are resolved, and Goodman Group and Digital Realty subsequently acquired a combined 9.9 per cent stake in NextDC in the period after the share price fall.

Source: Reuters  ·  9 November 2026
 New Builds

Lambda Plans to Invest $33.1 Billion in a Four Building AI Data Center Campus in Lowndes County, Alabama, Powered by Alabama Power and Expected to Break Ground in the First Quarter of 2027

Lambda announced on 5 November 2026 that it will develop a large scale AI data center campus near Burkville in Lowndes County, Alabama, in partnership with Cloverleaf Infrastructure, estimating a total investment of $33.1 billion and the creation of 1,000 jobs over the life of the project. The campus will be powered by Alabama Power pending regulatory approval, with construction expected to begin in the first quarter of 2027, and the company has committed $10 million through a Community Benefits Agreement with the Central Alabama Community Foundation.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  5 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Anthropic Agrees to Acquire $3.4 Billion Worth of Gas Fired Generators From OnePWR Solutions, Securing 1.7 Gigawatts of Caterpillar Equipment to Power Its AI Data Centers Off Grid

Anthropic agreed in late September 2026 to take over approximately $3.4 billion in gas fired power generator orders originally placed by Texas based supplier OnePWR Solutions, covering around 1.7 gigawatts of Caterpillar generation equipment along with the associated engineering and installation work, according to reporting by the Financial Times in November 2026. The deal is described as the largest direct investment in power generation equipment by an AI company to date, and comes as Anthropic prepares for a planned public offering, with the company flagging that reliance on natural gas exposes its data center buildout to volatile energy prices and community air quality concerns.

Source: Financial Times  ·  November 2026

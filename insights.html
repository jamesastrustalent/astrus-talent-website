Middle East Data Center Expansion Presses Ahead Despite Escalating Conflict With Iran

The Persian Gulf's wealthiest states are pressing ahead with plans for a massive data center buildout despite the industry becoming a repeated target in the conflict with Iran, with state backed firms in the UAE maintaining construction of the planned five gigawatt Abu Dhabi AI campus and adding security measures for workers following Iranian missile and drone attacks on cloud infrastructure across the region. Saudi Arabia is pursuing a parallel expansion through Humain and a growing network of international technology partnerships, with both governments treating AI data center capacity as a strategic national priority irrespective of the elevated security environment.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  4 September 2026

AWS and Humain Confirm 50 Megawatt Saudi AI Zone Target for 2028 as Kingdom's First Cloud Region Nears December Launch

Amazon Web Services and the Public Investment Fund backed Humain have confirmed that the first 50 megawatts of capacity at the Saudi AI Zone in Riyadh will be live by 2028, with approximately 150,000 AI chips including Nvidia GB300 accelerators and AWS Trainium processors set to be deployed in a combined investment exceeding $5.3 billion. AWS's first dedicated cloud infrastructure region in Saudi Arabia, offering three availability zones for customers across the Kingdom, remains on track to open in December 2026.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  5 September 2026

Digital Realty Forms Joint Venture With Rönesans Infrastructure to Develop Data Centers Across Türkiye

Digital Realty has formed a strategic joint venture with Rönesans Infrastructure to develop and operate next generation data centers across Türkiye, with the first campus to be a carrier neutral facility in Ankara designed to support more than 22 megawatts of IT capacity, targeted for completion in 2028. The partnership plans to expand into Istanbul following the Ankara opening, positioning Türkiye as a key digital hub at the intersection of European, Middle Eastern and Asian connectivity.

Source: GlobeNewswire  ·  14 September 2026

HSCALE, Backed by Bain Capital, Wins Over $1 Billion Contract With a Major US Cloud Provider for Its Spanish Data Center Campus

HSCALE, the EMEA focused data center platform launched by Bain Capital in 2025, has secured a contract worth more than $1 billion with an unnamed major US cloud provider for capacity at one of Spain's largest data center campuses currently under construction, with the first capacity available to the client in 2027. Spain's position as a gateway between European, African and Latin American digital markets has made it one of the most active data center development stories in Europe during 2026.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 September 2026

Thailand Suspends Construction on 49 Data Centers and Freezes Approvals for 117 More While Regulators Draft New Rules

Thailand has suspended construction on 49 data center projects and frozen approvals for a further 117 planned facilities following growing official concern over the industry's power and water demands and an environmental incident at a Bangkok site awaiting opening. The Board of Investment had already paused new approvals in April 2026 after 88 projects worth approximately 886 billion baht ($27 billion) were approved in the first half of the year; four working groups have been given one month to draft requirements covering electricity use, water consumption, site selection, safety and environmental impact.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  9 September 2026
 Capex Watch

NVIDIA Partners With Eight Australian Operators Targeting a Combined 2 Gigawatt AI Factory Buildout by 2027

NVIDIA announced a collaboration with eight Australian cloud and infrastructure partners including Sharon AI, NEXTDC and AirTrunk to develop land, power and shell capacity for multiple generations of NVIDIA DSX AI factories, targeting a combined buildout of up to 2 gigawatts by 2027. Sharon AI alone is deploying up to 68,000 NVIDIA GPUs with Quantum InfiniBand and Spectrum X networking, and across all eight operators the programme would more than double Australia's existing 1.6 gigawatt compute base.

Source: GlobeNewswire  ·  10 September 2026
 Market Intelligence

Growing Community Opposition to Data Centers Becomes a Defining Issue in the 2026 US Midterm Elections

Opposition to data centers has escalated from a niche local concern into a defining issue shaping the November 2026 US midterms, with over $31 million spent on campaign ads mentioning data centers and at least 75 projects worth a combined $130 billion blocked or delayed by local opposition in the first three months of 2026. An August survey by the Annenberg Public Policy Center found that approximately 61% of US adults oppose new data centers being built in their area, up 12 percentage points since earlier in the year, with the issue crossing party lines as both Republicans and Democrats express strong opposition.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  9 September 2026

Microsoft Plans to Triple Its Global Data Center Capacity to 38 Gigawatts by 2032 in Its Largest Ever AI Infrastructure Drive

Microsoft has announced plans to expand its global data center network from roughly 12 gigawatts of capacity today to more than 38 gigawatts by 2032, adding approximately 26 gigawatts of new compute capacity with a growing share dedicated to artificial intelligence accelerator hardware. The expansion is designed to overcome a computing shortage that forced the company to turn away some AI and cloud customers, with AI chip capacity expected to grow from around 2 gigawatts currently to approximately one third of the 2032 total.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  10 September 2026

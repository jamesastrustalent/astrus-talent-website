Market Intelligence

Trump's Threatened Overseas Data Center Tax Creates Uncertainty for Gulf AI Programmes as Saudi Arabia and Abu Dhabi Both Depend on US Technology Partnerships Covered by the Measure

Forbes reported on 21 November that President Trump's threatened tax on US companies building data centers outside the United States has created uncertainty for the Gulf's largest AI projects, including the AWS HUMAIN zone in Saudi Arabia and the Stargate UAE campus in Abu Dhabi, both agreed under close alignment with US technology firms. Analysts said the administration's position creates a tension between encouraging Gulf investment in US AI partnerships and penalising the same companies for building the capacity those partnerships require, as the UAE still needs US government authorisation to receive advanced Nvidia chips under existing export controls.

 Market Intelligence

Microsoft's Saudi Arabia East Datacenter Region Becomes Available to Customers This Month, With Three Azure Availability Zones Online in the Eastern Province Enabling Local Hosting of Cloud Workloads for Government and Enterprise Organisations

Microsoft confirmed that its Saudi Arabia East datacenter region is available to customers in November 2026, delivering three Azure Availability Zones in the Eastern Province as part of the company's US$5.2 billion commitment to Saudi AI and cloud infrastructure. The region is designed to let organisations meet data residency requirements by running eligible workloads within the Kingdom, though Microsoft noted that not every Azure service will be immediately available from within Saudi Arabia at launch.

Source: Microsoft News  ·  November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Spain Approves Royal Decree Requiring New Data Centers to Source at Least 80 Per Cent of Electricity From Newly Installed Renewables, With Industry Body Warning That Up to 90 Per Cent of €67 Billion in Planned Investment Could Relocate

Spain's Council of Ministers approved a royal decree on 21 November setting rules for new data centers to cover at least 80 per cent of their electricity from renewable capacity commissioned no more than 18 months before operations begin, including an hourly matching requirement. Industry association Spain DC warned that up to 90 per cent of the €67 billion committed to data center investment in Spain for 2026 to 2030 was at risk of relocating to countries with less restrictive energy rules, while the government said the requirements were necessary to control the environmental impact of AI infrastructure growth.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  21 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

ECB President Lagarde Calls on EU Public Funds to Take the First Losses on Data Center and Chip Plant Projects to Draw In Private Capital, Warning Europe Lacks the Compute Capacity to Meet Its Own AI Demand

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde told the Frankfurt European Banking Congress on 20 November that EU public funds should absorb first losses on major data center and chip plant projects to attract private investment, citing a projected need of €1.3 trillion to reach a 15 per cent share of global AI computing power by 2030. Lagarde, speaking at her final address to the conference before stepping down in February, said existing EU plans for chip factories do not go far enough and called for European level public and private partnerships modelled on approaches used to mobilise private capital at scale.

Source: CNBC  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

NTT Signs a Memorandum of Understanding With B.Grimm Power for a 400MW Data Center and AI Ecosystem Project in Thailand Valued at Approximately THB 113 Billion, Extending a Partnership Already in Place for the Bangkok 4 Facility

NTT Global Data Centers and Thai energy company B.Grimm Power signed a memorandum of understanding on 24 November to pursue a 400MW data center and AI infrastructure project in Thailand valued at approximately THB 113 billion, equivalent to around US$3.3 billion. The two companies already operate under a 100MW power purchase agreement for NTT's upcoming Bangkok 4 campus at the Amata City Chonburi industrial estate, and the new agreement would substantially expand their collaboration if progressed to binding terms.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  24 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Commerce Department Blacklists Singapore's Megaspeed and Six Related Entities Across Southeast Asia Over Alleged Nvidia Chip Diversion, Placing Approximately US$4.2 Billion in Regional Data Center Financing at Risk

The US Commerce Department added Megaspeed International and six affiliated entities operating across Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to its Entity List on 24 November, with all licence applications subject to a presumption of denial. The action follows a Justice Department investigation into suspected diversion of advanced Nvidia chips to China, with reporting that Apex Enterprise Solutions had prepaid approximately US$4.2 billion to Megaspeed and associated companies for computing capacity across Southeast Asia. Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said the country's investigation is ongoing and that Singapore does not want to serve as a conduit for circumventing US export controls.

Source: CNBC  ·  24 November 2026

TeraWulf Begins Vertical Construction at Its 480MW Justified Data Campus in Kentucky Leased to Anthropic Under a 20 Year Agreement Worth Approximately US$19 Billion in Contracted Revenue

TeraWulf announced on 20 November that structural steel is now going up at its Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky, marking the start of vertical construction at the facility it has leased to Anthropic under a 20 year agreement covering approximately 401MW of critical IT capacity. Fluor is the engineering and construction partner for a project estimated at between US$4 billion and US$4.5 billion excluding Anthropic's computing equipment, with first capacity expected in the second half of 2027 and full delivery by early 2028.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Loudoun County Virginia Adopts a 12 Month Pause on Legislative Data Center Applications, Temporarily Halting Development Approvals at the Heart of Data Center Alley as the County Reviews Its Comprehensive Plan

The Loudoun County Board of Supervisors voted 7 to 1 on 17 November to pause action on all legislative data center and electrical substation applications for up to 12 months, retroactive to 15 September 2026, while the county updates its comprehensive plan and zoning rules. The pause covers 33 active applications, though the county attorney found it cannot extend to by right administrative applications, meaning some development activity continues. Policy updates are targeted for adoption in July 2027.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  19 November 2026

Market Intelligence

Humain and Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund Sign SAR 2.5 Billion in Financing Agreements on 8 November to Fund GPU Procurement and Grid Infrastructure for the Kingdom's AI Data Center Programme

Humain and the National Infrastructure Fund (Infra) signed two financing agreements totalling SAR 2.5 billion ($667 million) on 8 November 2026, marking the first transactions under Infra's AI Data Center Financing Programme. The first facility of up to SAR 1.1 billion funds the procurement and deployment of GPUs representing approximately 25 megawatts of IT load, while the second of up to SAR 1.4 billion covers substations, transmission lines and grid connections for Humain's Saudi data center sites, with Aramco having completed its 49 per cent acquisition of Humain on 1 November and the company targeting 3 gigawatts of AI data center capacity by 2030.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  14 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Nvidia Interim Chief Executive Colette Kress Takes Over Following Jensen Huang's Resignation on 8 November, Raising Concerns Over the Reliability of GPU Supply to Gulf AI Programmes Totalling Tens of Billions of Dollars Across Saudi Arabia and the UAE

Fortune reported on 18 November 2026 that the leadership upheaval at Nvidia exposes how heavily the Gulf's AI ambitions depend on decisions made in Washington and Silicon Valley, with Humain contracted to deploy 27,000 GPUs and build 84 megawatts of computing capacity in Saudi Arabia, and Stargate UAE on course to build a 1 gigawatt AI cluster in Abu Dhabi. Nvidia's stock fell roughly 20 per cent during November as a result of the turmoil, though UAE retail investors were reported to have bought the dip, and the company's strong third quarter results provided some reassurance that the fundamental business remains intact.

Source: Fortune  ·  18 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Spain's Government Seeks Fast Track Approval for a Ministerial Decree Requiring Data Centers to Source 80 Per Cent of Their Electricity From Renewables on an Hourly Basis Before the 29 November General Election

The proposed decree would be passed without parliamentary approval and require facilities to match 80 per cent of their power consumption with renewable energy on an hourly basis, with industry body SpainDC warning the measure would create the most restrictive data center framework in the European Union. CBRE estimates developers have announced 10.5 gigawatts of capacity across Spain and Portugal, of which only about 4.5 gigawatts is considered realistically deliverable given grid and permitting constraints, while the Aragón regional government called the draft a de facto prohibition on new construction.

Source: CNBC  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Europe Must Triple Its Data Centre Capacity Within Five to Seven Years to Stay Competitive in AI, With Annual Investment Rising From 12 Billion Euros in 2020 to 25 Billion Euros in 2026 but Growing Protests and Grid Constraints Threatening Delivery of the Target

Euronews Verify reported on 17 November 2026 that the European Union is pursuing an ambition to triple its total data centre capacity, though environmental protests in Dublin, Helsinki and Amsterdam on 6 and 7 November, coordinated by a coalition of more than 100 organisations, have added political pressure to what are already significant grid and regulatory obstacles. Data centres accounted for 23 per cent of Ireland's total metered electricity consumption in 2025 according to official statistics, and opponents in several countries are pressing national governments for formal moratoriums on new construction.

Source: Euronews  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Banks and Private Credit Funds Behind Roughly 49 Billion Dollars of Asia Pacific Data Centre Financing Face Losses After US Commerce Department Licensing Rules Eliminate Key Chinese Revenue Streams for Regional Operators

Bloomberg reported on 17 November 2026 that lenders who financed data centre construction and GPU procurement across Southeast Asia now face a scenario in which borrowers' Chinese clients can no longer legally access computing capacity above a defined threshold, undermining revenue assumptions that underpinned loan agreements across the region. Talks on a 480 million dollar Nvidia GPU financing package for Singapore-based Aolani Cloud, which planned to install the hardware in a Malaysian data centre to serve ByteDance, stalled as a direct result of the new licensing requirement.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Thailand's Foreign Minister Says New US AI Chip Licensing Requirements Directly Threaten the Country's Ambition to Become a Regional Data Centre Hub as Governments Across Southeast Asia Seek Clarification on the Scope of the Commerce Department Rules

Bloomberg reported on 18 November 2026 that Thailand's foreign minister said in a television interview that the new US licensing requirements mean the country has to be more careful in its approach to attracting data centre investment, as the interim rule now covers Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia. Analysts warn the new framework could redirect investment flows across the region as developers reassess projects that were partly dependent on Chinese technology companies accessing compute capacity from facilities now subject to the licence requirement.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  18 November 2026

SpaceX Files Plans for a $24 Billion Data Center and Gas Fired Power Plant at Starbase on 444 Acres Near the Port of Brownsville, Sidestepping a 90 Day Development Moratorium Approved by Brownsville Days Earlier

SpaceX proposed the campus days after Brownsville's city commission unanimously approved a 90 day halt to data center development in the city, with the company having its preferred site removed from Brownsville's jurisdiction and annexed by the adjacent city of Starbase ahead of filing. The project forms part of a broader commitment of more than $74 billion in AI supercomputing campuses across Texas and Arizona, with the Texas site targeting more than 500 permanent jobs and a property tax abatement hearing scheduled for 9 December.

Source: Houston Chronicle  ·  11 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Voters Have Supported Limits on Data Center Development in 46 of the 47 Local Ballot Measures Decided in 2026, Including Outright Bans in California, Florida and Ohio, With Further Votes Due in December

Ballotpedia reported on 15 November 2026 that voters across the United States had decided 47 local ballot measures related to data center development during 2026, with 46 resulting in restrictions, moratoriums or outright bans, including five full bans approved in California, Florida and Ohio at November 3 elections. Analysts noted that the results will primarily affect projects planned from 2028 onwards, while developers in Arkansas, North Dakota and New Hampshire face further local votes in December.

Source: Ballotpedia  ·  15 November 2026

Market Intelligence

Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund Signs Two Financing Agreements With HUMAIN Worth SAR 2.5 Billion to Fund GPU Procurement for a 25MW AI Computing Facility and Grid Infrastructure Designed to Support Up to 1 Gigawatt of Future IT Capacity

Saudi Arabia's National Infrastructure Fund (Infra) and HUMAIN, the Public Investment Fund backed AI company, signed two financing agreements on 9 November totalling up to SAR 2.5 billion, equivalent to approximately $667 million. The first tranche of up to SAR 1.1 billion funds GPU procurement for an initial 25MW AI computing facility, while a second tranche of up to SAR 1.4 billion covers substations, transmission and grid connections designed to support up to 1 gigawatt of IT capacity, marking the first deals under Infra's AI Data Center Financing Program.

UAE Telecoms Operator du Launches National HyperAI Across Five Data Centers, Combining Sovereign AI Inference, GPU as a Service and Autonomous Agent Workflows Into a Single Platform Certified by the UAE Cyber Security Council

du, the UAE based telecoms and technology operator, announced National HyperAI on 16 November, a sovereign AI platform that bundles GPU as a service, model as a service and autonomous agent capabilities on the company's National Hypercloud infrastructure, certified by the UAE Cyber Security Council. The platform is designed to serve government ministries, national security authorities and enterprise customers who require AI computing with domestic data residency, targeting organisations across the UAE that cannot place sensitive workloads on international cloud infrastructure.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  16 November 2026

Leaked European Commission Draft Sets an Ambition to Triple EU Data Center Capacity to 37 Gigawatts Through Voluntary Tripartite Agreements While Rejecting Mandatory Green Energy Rules That Environmental Groups Warn Would Leave Grid Costs With Households

A draft European Commission proposal reported by Euronews on 26 November proposes roughly tripling EU data center capacity from approximately 12 gigawatts in 2025 to 37 gigawatts within five to seven years, using a framework of voluntary local agreements covering grid access, renewables, storage, flexibility and waste heat reuse. The draft does not impose binding green commitments on new facilities, drawing criticism from environmental campaigners who argue the approach transfers grid strain and rising electricity costs to households while giving operators no binding sustainability obligations.

Source: Euronews  ·  26 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Norway's Grid Operator Statnett Pauses New Data Center Grid Reservations Until August 2027 After Incoming Applications Totalling 34 Gigawatts Exceeded the Country's Entire National Peak Electricity Demand

Statnett, Norway's national transmission system operator, halted new grid capacity reservations for data centers on 9 November and said the pause would remain in place until at least 1 August 2027 while it develops updated technical connection requirements that the energy regulator must formally approve. Applications for large facilities seeking direct connections to the 420kV transmission grid are covered, including requests already on file, though Statnett will continue processing near term regional grid applications, as the country faces a queue of data center requests far exceeding the roughly 25 gigawatts of national peak demand.

Source: Unite AI  ·  9 November 2026

Alibaba Cloud Opens Its Sixth Malaysian Data Center and Third in Johor, Expanding Its Global Network to 109 Availability Zones as Malaysia Grows Into a Leading Southeast Asian Hub for Sovereign Cloud and AI Infrastructure

Alibaba Cloud inaugurated its sixth Malaysian data center on 19 November, adding the third facility at its Johor public cloud region to three already operating in Kuala Lumpur, and announced the opening at its Qwen Conference Malaysia 2026 alongside new AI model and coding capabilities. The expanded footprint targets Malaysian enterprises across banking, telecoms, digital payments, energy and creative industries that require locally hosted cloud and AI services with domestic data residency, as the country attracts growing volumes of hyperscale and cloud investment from US, Chinese and Japanese operators.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  19 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

All But Two of Nearly 600 Public Submissions Oppose Anthropic's Planned 1.44 Gigawatt Data Center Campus in Queensland's Western Downs, With Residents Raising Concerns Over Water Use, Electricity Costs and the Loss of Productive Farmland

Western Downs Regional Council received approximately 600 public submissions before its 11 November deadline on the Western Downs Digital Park, a planned 1.44 gigawatt campus roughly 250 kilometres west of Brisbane where Anthropic is the named anchor tenant, and all but two opposed the project or requested a delay pending further environmental assessment. Objections centred on water consumption, the impact on household electricity prices, construction traffic damage to rural roads and permanent farmland loss, with opposition rising sharply after developer Zerra named Anthropic as the primary tenant in September 2026.

Source: Reuters  ·  20 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Google Commits USD 17 Billion to Build Its First Wyoming Data Center on a 702 Megawatt Fuel Cell Campus Near Cheyenne, Partnering With Bloom Energy and AEP to Supply Gas Fired Power With Carbon Capture and Zero Water Cooling

Google announced on 5 November that it will invest $17 billion in a data center campus in Laramie County, Wyoming, its first in the state, powered by a 702 megawatt fuel cell plant contracted through American Electric Power and built using Bloom Energy equipment running on natural gas with on site carbon capture and storage. The company stated it will fully fund the electricity infrastructure required by the campus, which uses air cooling without water consumption, as it presses ahead with large scale AI infrastructure investment despite substantial midterm election backlash against data center development across the United States.

Source: Google Blog  ·  5 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Core Scientific Files a USD 1.3 Billion Planning Application for a 285MW Data Center Campus in Hunt County, Texas, With AMD Reported as an Anchor Tenant Taking 110 Megawatts Under a 15 Year Agreement With Delivery Starting in Early 2028

Core Scientific registered a $1.3 billion data center project in Hunt County, approximately 53 miles northeast of Dallas, covering a 341,325 square foot building on a 265 acre site in Quinlan with construction due to run from November 2026 through to September 2028 and an on site substation providing power from 2027. Data Center Dynamics reported the filing on 24 November, noting 285MW of leasable capacity planned across the campus, while Baxtel separately reported that AMD has agreed a 110 megawatt, 15 year lease as anchor tenant with delivery targeted for early 2028, though the filed documents themselves do not name any tenant.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  24 November 2026

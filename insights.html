Geopolitical Risk

Iran Warns It Will Target Gulf Data Centers and Undersea Cables if War With the US Resumes, Following Earlier Drone Strikes That Left AWS Unable to Restore Data From Its UAE Availability Zone

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei warned on 13 October 2026 that data centers, AI facilities and undersea cables across the UAE and the Strait of Hormuz would be attacked if conflict with the United States resumed. Earlier drone strikes this year damaged two AWS UAE data centers and a Bahrain facility, and Amazon Web Services has since confirmed it cannot restore resources hosted in its UAE availability zone mec1-az2, marking the first known permanent cloud data loss attributable to a conflict.

Source: Al Jazeera  ·  13 October 2026
 Sustainability

Abu Dhabi Data Center Operator Khazna Publishes Its First ESG Report and Targets 100% Carbon Free Electricity for Cooling Load by 2035 as the City's AI Infrastructure Buildout Accelerates

Khazna Data Centers published its first standalone environmental, social and governance report on 6 October 2026, setting an interim target of 50% carbon free electricity for mechanical cooling load by 2030 and 100% by 2035, while recording 246,468 MWh of grid electricity used for mechanical cooling across its fully owned Abu Dhabi facilities in 2025. The company's ESG director clarified that the 2035 goal applies specifically to cooling load rather than total site electricity consumption including compute, as rapid AI demand growth raises stakeholder expectations across the region.

Source: Gulf News  ·  14 October 2026
 New Builds

CX Tech Submits a £16.5 Billion Planning Application for a 1GW Data Center Campus at the Former Chapelcross Nuclear Power Station in Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland

CX Tech Limited lodged a Planning Permission in Principle application with Dumfries and Galloway Council in late September 2026 for eight data center buildings of approximately 120MW each at the decommissioned Chapelcross site near Annan, with a combined investment valued at £16.5 billion and a proposed construction timeline of 2027 to 2035. The developer says the campus would be powered entirely by direct and private wire connections to consented renewable generation and would create 750 construction jobs and 450 permanent roles, though Scottish Green MSPs have called the scheme reckless and the Scottish Government has said it will publish national guidance before any consent is granted.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  October 2026
 Capex Watch

French AI Infrastructure Firm Sesterce Plans a €10 Billion, 600MW AI Data Center Campus on a Former Paper Mill Site in Jämsä, Finland, Pledging to Match Every Megawatt Consumed With New Finnish Renewable Generation

Sesterce announced on 8 October 2026 that it would invest more than €10 billion in a phased AI data center campus at the former Kaipola paper mill in Jämsä, central Finland, with a 200MW first phase due to begin construction before the end of 2026 and a second phase raising total capacity to 600MW. The French company said it has secured an anchor customer, aims to commission the first phase in 2027, and has committed to enabling 1MW of new Finnish grid scale renewable generation for every megawatt the campus uses within 10 years, alongside a €10 million community investment fund for the Jämsä area.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  8 October 2026
 Capex Watch

DayOne Raises $3.67 Billion in New Malaysian Financing From Seven Banks Including DBS and Standard Chartered, Bringing Its Total Malaysia Facility to $7.2 Billion as Signed Bookings Reach 2.3GW

Singapore based data center developer DayOne secured an additional RM15 billion ($3.67 billion) in financing from a consortium of seven banks on 13 October 2026, structured as a dollar denominated term loan and an Islamic Murabahah facility. Total contracted financing for its Malaysian portfolio now stands at approximately $7.2 billion, with 1GW of IT power already earning revenue under long term customer agreements and 2.3GW in signed bookings.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  13 October 2026
 Market Intelligence

Nvidia Backed Australian Data Center Operator Firmus Abandons Its $5 Billion ASX Listing on Weak Institutional Demand, Citing an Implied Valuation of $30.6 Billion for a Company With Just Two Operating Sites

Firmus, the Nvidia backed Australian AI data center operator, withdrew its planned initial public offering on 9 October 2026 after failing to secure sufficient institutional orders at A$11 per share, a price implying an equity valuation of approximately $30.6 billion, roughly triple its August fundraising valuation. Investors cited limited operating track record, escrow terms that would have allowed existing shareholders to sell more than half their stock on listing day, and a development partner's exit from a major site agreement as the principal reasons for pulling orders; Firmus said it would seek private capital and consider alternative listing venues.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  9 October 2026
 New Builds

Meta Named as the Developer Behind a 3,000 Acre, $10 Billion Data Center Campus in Mason County, Kentucky, Securing a 40 Year Property Tax Break and Committing $115 Million to Local Road and Water Infrastructure

Meta was identified at a Mason County Fiscal Court meeting on 13 October 2026 as the company behind a roughly 3,000 acre campus in northern Kentucky, with a stated investment of $10 billion per phase and potential to reach up to 2.2GW of power capacity. A community benefits agreement approved by county commissioners includes $115 million for road upgrades, a new water treatment plant and pipelines, alongside a 40 year local property tax exemption under an Industrial Revenue Bond arrangement.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  15 October 2026
 Policy

Memphis City Council Delays Its Vote on a 12 Month Data Center Moratorium for the Second Time as Hundreds of Residents Protest Against xAI's Expanding Footprint in the City

The Memphis City Council postponed a vote on a proposed 12 month data center moratorium on 12 October 2026 for the second time, following mass attendance at the council chamber by residents calling for new restrictions near residential areas. The ordinance was proposed after a council member learnt via social media that xAI was constructing a fourth facility on land it already controls; it would pause approvals for new data centers while the city develops permanent zoning rules but would not affect any of xAI's existing sites, as Memphis joins Warren, Knox and Davidson counties in Tennessee in considering or enacting similar measures.

Source: Data Center Dynamics  ·  12 October 2026

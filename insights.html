Market Intelligence

Gulf Data Centre Association Finds the GCC Has Surpassed 1,055 Megawatts of Operational Capacity Across 114 Facilities as a Further Gigawatt Under Construction Marks More Than the Region Has Built in Its Entire History

The GDCA's 2026 market report, prepared by TechRE Consulting, found that existing Gulf data center capacity stands at 1,055.5 megawatts across 114 facilities in the six GCC countries, with a further 1 gigawatt now under construction. Despite the volume of investment agreements and AI infrastructure announcements across Saudi Arabia and the UAE, the association found that completions slowed sharply in 2026 as Saudi project delays contrasted with continued UAE expansion.

Source: The Tech Capital  ·  November 2026
 New Builds

Saudi AI Company HUMAIN and National Infrastructure Fund Infra Sign a US$667 Million Financing Agreement to Fund Chip Procurement and Power Infrastructure in a First for Saudi Arabia's AI Data Center Programme

HUMAIN and Infra signed financing agreements worth US$667 million in Riyadh on 8 November 2026, allocating approximately US$293 million to procure AI chips and US$373 million to build substations and grid connections designed to support up to 1 gigawatt of IT load. The deal is the first executed under Infra's AI Data Center Financing Programme and forms part of a broader SAR 4.5 billion framework covering up to 250 megawatts of capacity announced at Davos in January 2026.

Source: The Tech Capital  ·  11 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Thousands Rally in Dublin and Helsinki as Stop Dirty Data Centres Protesters Enter a Microsoft Construction Site in Amsterdam and Call for a European Moratorium on New AI Infrastructure Builds

Approximately 2,000 people marched in Dublin and 1,300 gathered in Helsinki on 7 November 2026, with simultaneous actions in cities across Finland, as the Stop Dirty Data Centres coalition demanded a halt to new large data center development pending community consultation. In Amsterdam, around 100 demonstrators entered a Microsoft leased data center construction site the previous evening; campaigners highlighted that Irish data centers already account for 23 per cent of the country's total metered electricity consumption.

Source: Bloomberg  ·  7 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Euronews Verify Finds Europe's Ambition to Triple Data Center Capacity Within Five to Seven Years Is Colliding With Rising Community Opposition Across 15 Countries and With Grid and Sustainability Constraints

Annual investment in European data center projects has risen from 12 billion euros in 2020 to approximately 25 to 26 billion euros in 2026 as the EU pursues a plan to reduce dependence on US and Chinese technology infrastructure by tripling its data center capacity. The IEA estimates data centers will account for 10 per cent of additional EU electricity demand by 2030, a figure regulators are attempting to manage through a new electronic sustainability labelling scheme, with minimum performance rules not expected until 2027.

Source: Euronews  ·  17 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

NextDC Pauses All Uncontracted Data Center Projects After Power Delays and Community Opposition Force a Profit Warning at Its Flagship M3 Melbourne Campus and Send the Company's Share Price Down 42 Per Cent

NextDC announced on 9 November 2026 that it would halt all development projects without signed customer contracts, following a decision by electricity distributor Jemena to suspend network connection works at its West Footscray site in Melbourne, where the M3 campus expansion had already triggered a profit downgrade. CEO Craig Scroggie said the company could not resume work until it had resolved the underlying problems and rebuilt trust with its stakeholders, as Goodman Group and Digital Realty finalised an AUD 715 million stake acquisition in the company on the same day.

Source: The Star  ·  9 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

Cushman and Wakefield Finds 67 Per Cent of Asia Pacific Data Center Sites Face Moderate to High Climate Risk as Operators Reassess Long Term Infrastructure Planning Across 30 Cities in 13 Markets

A Cushman and Wakefield report published on 18 November 2026, developed with climate analytics firm Jupiter Intelligence, assessed more than 300 data center sites across the Asia Pacific region and found that the fastest growing markets are not consistently those with the lowest climate exposure. The analysis concluded that historical climate conditions are no longer sufficient inputs for long term infrastructure planning, and that operators are increasingly factoring climate intelligence into site selection, engineering design and operational decisions.

Source: Cushman and Wakefield  ·  18 November 2026
 New Builds

SpaceX Announces Plans for More Than US$74 Billion in AI Data Center Campuses in Texas and Arizona as It Seeks Approvals for Sites That Could Collectively Draw Up to 6 Gigawatts of Electricity at Full Build Out

SpaceX revealed plans on 12 November 2026 to invest approximately US$24 billion near its Starbase facility in the Rio Grande Valley of Texas and US$50 billion on a campus in Cochise County, Arizona, in what would rank among the largest private infrastructure commitments in US history. The Arizona project requires county approval and is expected to generate 1,000 permanent jobs; the Texas site is set to generate its own power and direct US$220 million to expand water supply in nearby Brownsville, with a separate approvals hearing scheduled for 9 December 2026.

Source: The Hill  ·  12 November 2026
 Market Intelligence

US Senate Passes Bipartisan Permitting Bill Requiring Data Center Developers to Cover Grid Upgrade Costs as a Battle Over Banning Data Centers on Federal Land Shapes the Debate Ahead of a House Vote

The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act by 62 votes to 33 on 19 November 2026, with 14 Democrats in favour, including a provision requiring data center developers to pay for electricity grid upgrades rather than passing costs on to ratepayers. Critics argued the bill would accelerate development by streamlining environmental permitting for energy infrastructure, and an amendment seeking to ban data centers on federal land was not brought to a vote, leaving the question unresolved as the bill moves to the House after the Thanksgiving recess.

Source: Inside Climate News  ·  19 November 2026
